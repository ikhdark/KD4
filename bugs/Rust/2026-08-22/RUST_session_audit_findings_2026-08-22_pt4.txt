## Conclusion

Yes—explicit epistemic state would let kda safely attempt more advanced analysis. But kda should not globally replace booleans with a single `Proven | Disproven | Unknown | PartiallySupported` enum.

The stronger architecture is:

1. Track positive evidence as `must` and `may`.
2. Track completeness separately, scoped to the affected fact family, place, phase, and outcome.
3. Derive `Proven | Disproven | Unknown` for a specific proposition at the query or diagnostic boundary.
4. Treat partial support as evidence, not as a fourth truth value.

This follows the abstract-interpretation model: an analysis result is a sound approximation that can retain useful facts without claiming complete knowledge. [Cousot and Cousot’s foundational paper](https://cs.nyu.edu/~pcousot/COUSOTpapers/POPL77.shtml) explicitly frames useful static analysis this way.

## kda already has most of the ingredients

The premise that kda is currently purely boolean is not quite accurate. It already has several independent uncertainty models:

- Semantic queries distinguish `Exact`, `Conditional`, `Ambiguous`, `Incomplete`, and `NotFound` in [semantic_query/model.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/semantic_query/model.rs:153).
- Relational analysis uses `Proved`, `Refuted`, `Unknown`, and `Unreachable` in [relational_proof.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/relational_proof.rs:58).
- General summaries have `must`/`may` facts plus detailed unknown boundaries in [semantic_summary.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/semantic_summary.rs:376).
- Effects track incompleteness per semantic family, explicitly so unresolved persistence behavior does not destroy lifecycle or blocking facts, in [effects.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/effects.rs:87).
- Dynamic target resolution retains both known targets and uncertainty reasons in [dynamic_targets.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/dynamic_targets.rs:154).
- The MIR protocol already requires unsupported MIR, external bodies, dynamic dispatch, alias escapes, and unusable mappings to become explicit precision losses—not absence—in [mir-fact-protocol.md](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/docs/mir-fact-protocol.md:32).

So the opportunity is to unify these as a contract between analyses and diagnostics. The danger is in checks that consume a partial graph or summary and discard its incompleteness.

## Recommended result model

For a proposition `P`, expose a derived verdict:

- `Proven`: sufficient sound evidence establishes `P`.
- `Disproven`: a sound counterexample establishes `¬P`.
- `Unknown`: neither side has sufficient evidence because of a relevant precision boundary.
- `Unreachable`: separate from truth; useful operationally and already present in kda.

Underneath that verdict, retain something like:

```text
must facts
may facts
unknown boundaries:
  reason
  affected capability/fact family
  affected places
  phase and outcomes
  callsite or expansion
  world assumptions
  analysis bounds
witness or counterexample
```

“Partially supported” should normally mean:

```text
known may-facts + an unknown remainder
```

For example, two known `CoreToolRuntime` implementations plus an open extension producer should produce two known possible targets and an `OpenWorld` boundary. That is more informative than a standalone `PartiallySupported` value.

A four-valued evidence model becomes useful only if kda must reconcile contradictory sources—for example, stale MIR claiming an edge exists while current HIR claims it does not. Then `both supported and refuted` should be called `Conflict`, not partial support. Belnap-style four-valued systems distinguish incomplete from contradictory evidence, but that complexity is probably unnecessary until kda combines independently versioned evidence artifacts. [This treatment of four epistemic states](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~tsoukias/papers/ddt18.pdf) explains the distinction.

## Propagation through call summaries

The key rules should be:

- Sequence: preserve already-proven facts. An opaque persistence call should not erase a previously proven cancellation fact. Propagate its unknown boundary only into dependent capabilities, places, phases, and outcomes.
- Branch join: `must` is the intersection of all feasible, complete alternatives; `may` is their union. An unknown alternative removes affected `must` guarantees but does not erase known `may` witnesses.
- Dynamic dispatch:
  - Closed target set: join all concrete summaries.
  - Open target set: join known targets, then add an unknown alternative.
  - A fact is `must` only if every possible target establishes it and the set is closed.
  - A known target can establish `may` even while additional targets remain unknown.
- External call: apply an exact configured contract if available; otherwise mark only the contract’s relevant effect families unknown.
- Recursion and limits: return `Unknown(RecursiveWidening)` or `Unknown(AnalysisLimit)`, distinct from semantic open-world uncertainty.
- Outcomes: compose normal return, error, unwind, cancellation, and drop separately. Unknown unwind behavior should not globally poison unrelated normal-path facts.
- Aliasing: attach uncertainty to affected places. An escaped argument should not make every local value unknown.

kda’s general semantic summaries already implement much of this shape. They are a better foundation than introducing a simpler global tri-state.

## Diagnostic policy

The practical rule is:

> Unknown suppresses a definitive diagnostic only when that diagnostic depends on proving absence or exhaustiveness.

| Proposition | Proven | Disproven | Unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| “A violating path exists” | Concrete feasible witness | Complete search with no witness | No witness, but search incomplete |
| “Every path satisfies the contract” | Complete coverage, all paths satisfy | Concrete counterexample path | No counterexample, but coverage incomplete |

Consequences for current checks:

- Required mediation: a concrete path reaching `handle_any_tool` before `ToolRegistry::dispatch_any_with_terminal_outcome` is enough to diagnose. Unknown elsewhere does not invalidate that witness.
- Trait `must-not-reach`: likewise, a concrete forbidden path is sufficient.
- Trait `must-reach`: absence requires a complete graph. Currently [trait_effect_envelope.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/trait_effect_envelope.rs:315) diagnoses simply when `reached` is false; an unresolved dynamic edge should instead make that result unknown.
- Registration: missing or unreachable registration requires complete registration collection and reachability. The current traversal uses only resolved production calls in [registration.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/registration.rs:616), yet emitted issues are labeled `certain`. This is the clearest current beneficiary.
- Variant flow: “no consumer exists” in [variant_flow.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/variant_flow.rs:246) requires completeness unless the contract explicitly defines an authored-source-only universe.
- Ordering: a concrete path that publishes before commit remains diagnostic even if other branches are unknown. If an unresolved node might contain the prerequisite, the verdict is unknown—as the existing ordering policy already recognizes in [semantic_effect_policy.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/semantic_effect_policy.rs:223).

## When unknown is useful to Codex

Unknown should be surfaced when:

- Codex explicitly asks for a proof or certification.
- It blocks a deny-level architectural or security conclusion.
- KD4 config declares completeness itself mandatory, as its plan-ordering checks do.
- A change introduces a new unknown boundary or increases unknown coverage.
- The boundary is actionable: missing contract, open dispatch producer, unsupported macro, alias escape, stale MIR, or exceeded bound.

Ordinary lint output should not emit thousands of generic unknown warnings. Query, explanation, and change-review modes should show them; standard lint mode should normally aggregate or suppress them unless configured as strict.

## Preventing unknown from consuming the analyzer

- Never allow a bare `Unknown`; require a reason and scope.
- Preserve known evidence alongside unknown.
- Aggregate repeated unknowns by their root boundary.
- Distinguish `not found`, `not analyzed`, `inconclusive`, and `conflicting`.
- Allow per-check policies: `definitive-only`, `report-inconclusive`, and `require-complete-proof`.
- Track unknown coverage as telemetry and regressions, not merely raw counts.
- Use explicit closed-world declarations and dependency contracts to turn high-value unknowns into proofs.
- Keep exact local structural checks boolean internally. Duplicate identifiers, direct forbidden construction, and fully collected signature checks do not need epistemic ceremony.

## Confidence and provenance

Provenance should accompany every nontrivial proof, counterexample, and unknown. Confidence should not substitute for proof status.

Useful provenance includes:

- semantic, syntax, heuristic, MIR, or configured-contract origin;
- witness path or counterexample;
- source configuration, features, target, and world-boundary assumptions;
- first unknown callsite;
- affected fact families and places;
- recursion/candidate/state limit reached.

Labels such as `certain`, `high`, and `likely` are useful only for ranking heuristic evidence. They should never turn an incomplete absence search into a definite violation.

Overall: explicit epistemic state is worth formalizing, especially for registration, required effects, whole-workspace reachability, dynamic dispatch, typestate/lifecycle, security flow, cancellation, and future resource/deadlock analyses. The best move is to standardize the richer model kda already contains and require consumers to respect completeness—not to force every checker through a new four-value enum.

# Recommendation

The practical end state is not “more lints.” It is a bounded semantic contract engine and semantic change oracle for Rust workspaces.

Its core job should be:

> Given a workspace world, a proposed patch, and project contracts, determine which semantic guarantees were preserved, violated, weakened, or rendered unknown—and provide a concrete witness for every conclusion.

That would materially improve autonomous development because an agent could ask before editing:

- What owns this resource?
- Which consumers depend on this enum or state transition?
- What must happen before success is published?
- Which effects can this wrapper transitively cause?
- Where did this authority or identity originate?
- Which exits leave obligations outstanding?

And after editing:

- Did the patch change observable behavior?
- Which guarantees became weaker or unknown?
- Which tests and review surfaces are actually implicated?
- Is the change safe only in one Cargo/configuration world?
- What counterexample explains the failure?

This is ambitious but still practical because it uses bounded domains, explicit contracts, finite summaries, and explicit unknowns—not general theorem proving.

## Assessment of the current architecture

kda already contains most of the right ideas, but they exist as a semantic archipelago.

The repository now has roughly 347 Rust source files and 290,000 lines, with more than 220 modules declaring lint IDs. Approximately 218 modules access the analysis snapshot directly, 260 perform syntax descendant walks, and pervasive string matching remains part of the semantic implementation. That is too much independently implemented interpretation for a 0.1 analyzer.

The main architectural problems are:

- Several competing definitions of identity, semantic facts, relations, certainty, proof status, and semantic families.
- Several CFG, effect, obligation, provenance, and revision models that overlap without sharing one canonical fact model.
- The central context caches some useful indices, but much of the analyzer remains a collection of independent workspace scans.
- Query assembly manually dispatches into many domain-specific builders rather than querying a common semantic database.
- The newer workspace semantic summary is conceptually strong, but my search found no production consumer outside its construction path.
- The relational theory and proof modules appear similarly isolated from production analysis.
- The revision and diff modules duplicate types such as `SemanticFamily` and `RelationIdentity`.
- The regression corpus evaluator classifies supplied observations, but does not itself establish that fixtures are being executed by the intended semantic engine.

Parts worth preserving as foundations include:

- rust-analyzer resolution and workspace loading.
- `AnalysisSnapshot` and `ProgramIndex` as transitional collectors.
- The external, toolchain-matched [MIR fact protocol](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/docs/mir-fact-protocol.md).

- Explicit coverage and uncertainty in semantic revision artifacts.
- KD4’s declarative contracts and historical regression corpus.

## 1. Ideal semantic model

The canonical model should have seven concepts.

### World

Every fact belongs to an explicit `WorldId`:

```text
revision
× target triple
× Cargo profile
× enabled features
× cfg values
× toolchain/compiler identity
× dependency graph
× contract-set version
```

Facts from different worlds must never be silently combined. This directly addresses profile drift, platform-only behavior, host/remote namespace confusion, and configuration-specific registration gaps.

A multi-world query can compare worlds, but the comparison must be explicit.

### Semantic identity

Identity needs three related but distinct forms:

1. **Compiler identity**
   Crate disambiguator, definition path hash, body identity, instance/substitution, and world. Used for exact within-revision reasoning.

2. **Revision-stable entity identity**
   Used for semantic diffing. Based on owner structure, kind, signature, logical role, and fingerprints—not source coordinates. Cross-revision matching carries a confidence and cannot silently claim equivalence.

3. **Domain identity**
   A declared recipe for logical resources: session key, rollout ID, filesystem object, cache epoch, process group, permission scope, transaction, remote upload, and so on.

Domain identity must distinguish:

- A pathname from the filesystem object it locates.
- A locator from the semantic identity of the loaded skill.
- A cache key from the epoch under which its value was established.
- A child PID from a process group or job object.
- A host path from a path inside another execution environment.

“Same string” must never be the default definition of “same thing.”

### Typed semantic facts and relations

All collectors should emit into one typed fact store. A fact contains:

```text
world
subject / relation / object
control point or region
outcome
must | may
precision and completeness
evidence and derivation
dependencies
```

Relations should be typed, not free-form predicate strings. Important families include:

- calls, dispatches-to, wraps, mediates;
- derives-from, projects-to, aliases, denotes;
- owns, borrows, delegates, consumes, revokes;
- creates, mutates, persists, publishes, deletes;
- precedes, dominates, postdominates, happens-before;
- activates, registers, serializes-as, decodes-as;
- opens, discharges, transfers, or abandons an obligation.

Extensibility should come through registered relation families and theory packs, not more unrelated `SemanticFact` enums.

### Finite abstract state

The local interpreter should use a deliberately small product domain:

- small constants and enum variants;
- `Option`/`Result` state;
- intervals and affine relationships;
- finite sets and cardinality bounds;
- symbolic places and bounded alias partitions;
- typestate;
- ownership/capability tokens;
- epochs and freshness;
- locksets and task identity;
- outstanding obligations;
- path conditions limited to predicates the selected domains understand.

When a construct falls outside those domains, the result becomes unknown. It should not trigger uncontrolled symbolic expansion.

### Callable summaries

A summary must be a finite transfer relation, not merely a set of effects:

```text
(input symbolic state, aliases, capabilities, obligations)
    ->
for each outcome:
    output state
    must/may effects
    created/transferred/discharged obligations
    capability flow
    ordering edges
    spawned or escaped work
    unknown boundaries
```

Outcomes should include normal return, typed error/variant outcomes where useful, unwind, cancellation, suspension, termination, and escape.

Summaries are parameter-relative so they compose across callers. Recursive components use bounded fixed-point iteration with widening and explicit convergence status.

### Contracts

Contracts should be separate from collected facts. The engine needs a compact set of reusable contract forms:

- State machines and legal transitions.
- Outcome-indexed obligations.
- Ownership and capability policies.
- Mediation and authority-flow constraints.
- Ordering, dominance, and atomic-region constraints.
- Effect envelopes and noninterference.
- Projection, parity, registration, and schema closure.
- Quantitative limits and affine bounds.
- Relational equations over collections and materialized views.
- Cross-revision guarantee requirements.

A project contract identifies semantic roles and equations. It should not encode the implementation shape expected to satisfy them.

### Evidence and proof status

Use one proof vocabulary everywhere:

- `Satisfied`: established within the declared model and coverage.
- `Violated`: a concrete counterexample or contradictory relation was found.
- `Unknown`: the engine crossed an unsupported boundary, exceeded a bound, lacked a world, or failed to converge.
- `NotApplicable`: the contract’s activation conditions do not hold.

Each result must also say how it was established:

- Compiler-resolved.
- MIR-derived.
- HIR-derived approximation.
- Contract assumption.
- External/tool-owned semantic fact.
- Runtime observation.
- Cross-revision match with stated confidence.

Avoid a bare `Proved` flag. A syntax heuristic and an exhaustive finite-state result do not justify the same claim.

## 2–4. Major reasoning layers, composition, and KD4 bug coverage

The layers should compose in one direction:

```text
Cargo worlds + HIR/MIR + project contracts + optional runtime evidence
                              │
                    canonical semantic database
                              │
        local CFG, symbolic places, provenance and abstract state
                              │
               per-outcome interprocedural summaries
                              │
 protocols / obligations / ordering / capability / theory packs
                              │
                revision artifact and semantic diff
                              │
          agent queries, proof receipts, diagnostics and gates
```

| Layer | Responsibility | Representative KD4 bug classes captured |
|---|---|---|
| 1. World, identity, and provenance | Resolve what an entity is, which world it belongs to, and where values or authority originated. | Path-string versus same-file identity; artifact reopened by pathname; symlink identity errors; stale cache values across repository epochs; host `HOME` mixed with remote filesystem namespace; wrong permission-profile provenance; cache keys missing semantically relevant dimensions. |
| 2. Structural relation graph | Model call/dispatch, registration, schema, projection, mediation, ownership, and consumer relations. | Tool/profile registration closure; enum/SQL vocabulary drift; new event variants not handled by all consumers; execution-route parity; public boundary leakage; bypassed tool registries; fields dropped while reconstructing persistent state. |
| 3. Local path-sensitive abstract interpretation | Reason over branches, early exits, matches, loops with bounds, return outcomes, drops, and small numerical domains. | Cleanup skipped by early return; finalization failure reported as success; no-op mutation still invalidates cache state; affine textarea bounds; missing partial-result propagation; cancellation or error paths bypassing required work; limit checks applied to the wrong quantity. |
| 4. Interprocedural effects and capability flow | Compose summaries through wrappers, traits, callbacks, dispatch, spawn, and bounded recursion. | Wrapper effect-envelope violations; cancellation not propagated through helpers; “do not run tests” bypassed through command wrappers; rollout noninterference; spawned work escaping its intended lifetime; authority delegated to an unauthorized sink; lifecycle behavior hidden behind a convenience API. |
| 5. Protocol, obligation, transaction, and ordering solver | Track resources and state machines; require obligations on relevant outcomes; verify partial orders and transactional visibility. | Registered processes orphaned on `Drop`; callbacks left outstanding; cleanup/reap missing after cancellation; terminalization omitted; marker deleted before durable log update; publish/notify before value installation; multi-resource writes lacking rollback; partial remote uploads; missing parent-directory durability; rename/delete visibility windows. |
| 6. Bounded interference and quantitative analysis | Model logical resources, task boundaries, locksets, epochs, bounded interference classes, total budgets, and progress bounds. | Singleflight remove-before-publish races; active cache identities evicted and double-counted; non-atomic read/modify/write; global suppression conflating independent failures; per-item deadlines exceeding an aggregate deadline; pagination caps and cursor cycles; unbounded wait after kill; concurrent prefetchers each consuming the full global budget. |
| 7. Domain relational theory packs | Supply small decidable algebras for recurring semantic equations. | Contextual path equality/hash coherence; snapshot plus replay buffer as current materialized view; minimal delta/frame updates; filter-before-rank; projection lineage; model-switch inverse effects; coverage of a required capability set. |
| 8. Semantic differencing and consequence analysis | Compare guarantees, effects, schemas, protocols, and coverage across revisions; compute affected consumers and tests. | A patch adding an enum variant without updating all projections; a field no longer preserved; a success path losing cleanup; a wrapper acquiring a new effect; a target profile becoming incomplete; behavior becoming unknown because a call now crosses an unresolved boundary. |

The most valuable composition rule is that higher layers do not re-parse syntax. A protocol solver asks the summary/query layer for transitions and outcomes. A semantic-diff collector asks for canonical relations. A lint asks the contract engine for a proof result.

The [KD4 relational counterexamples](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/docs/kd4-relational-counterexamples.md) already point in this direction: several apparently unrelated bugs collapse into a few theory packs once roles and relations are explicit. ## 5. What should remain deliberately unsupported

kda should stop before it becomes a Rust verifier or model checker.

Deliberately unsupported areas should include:

- General heap shape and unrestricted pointer/alias analysis.
- Full reasoning about `unsafe` and the Rust memory model.
- Exhaustive async schedule or thread interleaving exploration.
- Arbitrary liveness proofs.
- General symbolic execution or unbounded loop reasoning.
- Nonlinear arithmetic, floating-point correctness, and broad SMT solving.
- Arbitrary string, regular-language, shell, SQL, Git, or protocol semantics.
- Full filesystem, network, database, and process semantics without declared models.
- Reflection, runtime code loading, FFI, or opaque dynamic dispatch without summaries.
- Exhaustive analysis of the Cargo feature powerset.
- Dependency internals when no compatible summary or source is available.
- Proving absence of effects after crossing an unknown boundary.
- Treating runtime traces as universal proof.

Some of these should be delegated to semantic owners. KD4’s shell parsing, Git interpretation, SQL behavior, OS process semantics, and cryptographic behavior are frequently better checked through external tools, tests, or small explicit models.

Concurrency analysis should stop at logical resources, epochs, task ownership, locksets, happens-before edges, bounded race templates, and protocol transitions. It should not enumerate schedules.

The economic cutoff should be explicit. A new abstract domain or theory pack is justified when it:

- Captures several independent historical failures or enables a core agent query.
- Has a finite representation and predictable convergence.
- Produces actionable witnesses.
- Maintains low false-positive rates by returning unknown when evidence is incomplete.
- Can be incrementally recomputed over a changed dependency closure.

A bespoke theory for one unusual bug should normally remain a project contract or test. If it requires modeling most of an external system, the analysis has stopped paying for itself.

## 6. Query infrastructure versus lint infrastructure

Most of the future system belongs in query infrastructure.

Query infrastructure should own:

- Worlds and activation conditions.
- Stable and compiler identities.
- Source and runtime evidence.
- Typed relations and dependency tracking.
- Call and dispatch resolution.
- CFG and symbolic places.
- Provenance and alias partitions.
- Effect and outcome summaries.
- Capability flow.
- Protocol and obligation execution.
- Ordering and interference reasoning.
- Coverage, precision, uncertainty, and derivation chains.
- Semantic revision artifacts and differencing.
- Incremental invalidation.
- Counterexample extraction and explanation.

Its primary API should answer questions such as:

```text
effects_of(entity, outcome)
paths_to(effect_or_sink)
obligations_at(exit)
origin_of(value_or_capability)
consumers_of(entity_or_variant)
state_transitions(resource_identity)
ordering_between(event_a, event_b)
changed_guarantees(before, after)
impacted_contracts(change)
explain(result)
```

Lint infrastructure should be thin. It owns:

- Contract selection and configuration.
- Severity and gate policy.
- Human-facing diagnostic wording.
- Suppression and baseline policy.
- Whether incomplete coverage is itself forbidden for a specific contract.
- Suggested remediation and output formatting.

No lint should independently walk a syntax tree to rediscover calls, exits, provenance, or effects. If a lint needs a missing semantic fact, the fact belongs in query infrastructure.

The current manual query routing should ultimately disappear. Queries should be declarative requests against the semantic database, with dependencies recorded automatically. Likewise, `AnalysisContext` should evolve from a bag of lazily built indices into a facade over a revision-scoped query engine.

## 7. Smallest non-rewriting architectural sequence

This can be reached through six upgrades using a strangler approach.

### 1. Establish the canonical semantic kernel

Choose one definition each for:

- `WorldId`
- `EntityId`
- domain identity
- fact/relation
- symbolic place
- evidence/provenance
- certainty/precision/coverage
- proof result
- dependency/invalidation key

Version this schema. Adapt existing `ProgramIndex`, semantic facts, revision artifacts, and diagnostics into it. Do not allow new analyzers to introduce competing fact or proof types.

This must come first; otherwise every later subsystem will require another rewrite.

### 2. Replace pass orchestration with a semantic query database

Make collectors populate or derive canonical facts through dependency-tracked queries. Cache by world and entity, not merely by whole-run analysis object.

Existing lint modules can continue operating through adapters during migration, but reusable queries become the only supported route for new reasoning.

The regression corpus should run real fixtures through these queries and record coverage and unknown causes, rather than merely classify externally supplied lint observations.

### 3. Converge on one body representation with precision tiers

Create one canonical body/control-flow model covering places, calls, branches, outcomes, drops, unwind, suspension, spawn, and source witnesses.

Use two evidence tiers:

- HIR/rust-analyzer for fast, broadly available analysis.
- Toolchain-matched MIR facts for exact cleanup, drop, coroutine, and lower-level control-flow questions.

They must populate the same schema. MIR should refine HIR facts, not create a second analysis universe. The existing external MIR protocol is the correct process boundary.

### 4. Make callable summaries the interprocedural center

Promote the current semantic-summary concepts into the canonical summary solver. Merge effects, value provenance, resource obligations, async relationships, drop behavior, and dynamic dispatch into its transfer representation.

After this step:

- No separate transitive effect graph.
- No separate summary format for each contract family.
- No protocol solver performing its own call traversal.
- No effect claim without an outcome, precision, provenance, and unknown-boundary record.

### 5. Build one contract engine with reusable theory packs

Implement state machines, obligations, capability policies, ordering, quantitative constraints, and relational equations as consumers of the same summaries and local abstract states.

Theory packs should remain small:

- Contextual identity and projection.
- Finite collection/view algebra.
- Affine and cardinality refinements.
- Lifecycle/transaction algebra.
- Bounded interference and epochs.

Existing KD4 contracts in [kda.toml](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/kda.toml) should be ported by semantic family, not lint by lint.

### 6. Put semantic diff at the center, then retire legacy analyzers

Generate revision artifacts directly from the canonical semantic database. Compare guarantees, effects, relations, coverage, and proof status—not syntax or diagnostic lists.

Once a contract family is backed by the new substrate:

- Redirect its queries and lints.
- Run old and new implementations against the KD4 corpus.
- Compare witnesses, false positives, unknown rates, and runtime.
- Remove the old model and scans when equivalence is acceptable.

At the end, the analyzer execution plan should contain only a few phases: collect, solve, evaluate contracts, compare revisions, and render. Hundreds of independent semantic passes should no longer be part of the architecture.

## Verdict

Yes—a small, domain-aware escape-summary layer would provide enough leverage to belong in `kda`’s semantic core.

The useful question is not the compiler-style “does this value outlive its stack frame?” KD4’s recurring question is:

> Which subsystem becomes responsible for this value or capability, through what channel, and beyond which lifecycle boundary may it remain usable?

That distinction is necessary for at least five concrete KD4 protocols involving live processes, execution permits, terminal tasks, pending request waiters, and step-scoped capability snapshots.

## 1. Spawned process custody

KD4 explicitly retains every process produced by sandbox retries until startup is either committed to the process store/ledger or cancelled and cleaned up. See [`PendingSpawnRegistration`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/unified_exec/mod.rs:151), [`PendingProcessRegistration`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/unified_exec/process_manager.rs:262), and the store handoff around [`store_process`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/unified_exec/process_manager.rs:949).

Tests cover cancellation during construction and retention after failed termination in [`process_tests.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/unified_exec/process_tests.rs:291).

**Semantic escape**

A live child process escapes when custody moves from the startup attempt into a longer-lived owner: the pending registration, process store, ledger, or a task responsible for exit observation and cleanup.

An `Arc` clone alone is not the escape. The escape occurs when a strong reference crosses into an owner that can keep the process alive after the startup operation ends.

**Channels**

- Storage in the pending-registration collection
- Process-store insertion
- Ledger ownership transfer
- Return from a constructor/helper
- Capture by cleanup or exit-watcher tasks
- Channel send to a process manager
- Strong versus weak `Arc` storage

**Behavioral importance**

A missed or malformed handoff can:

- Orphan an OS process after request cancellation
- Drop the only live custodian prematurely
- Lose the process ID or ledger reservation
- Leave a resumable process unreachable
- Retain a session accidentally through a strong back-reference

**Minimum analysis**

- Track configured process, reservation, and registration origins.
- Follow moves and `Arc` clones through local places and bounded helper summaries.
- Recognize registration, store insertion, ledger transfer, commit, and cleanup as distinct destinations/actions.
- Relate the handoff to cancellable awaits and exit paths.
- Use MIR only when a diagnostic requires path-complete drop or cancellation ordering.

**Lexical vs. ownership vs. domain**

- Lexical: detects that the value survives its constructor frame, but labels both correct and incorrect retention alike.
- Ownership: distinguishes a strong custodian from a weak reference or borrow.
- Lifetime-domain: distinguishes the permitted attempt → pending-registration → session-store transition from an orphaning or untracked transition.

**Valuable diagnostics**

- Live process reaches a cancellable await before pending registration.
- Registration is committed without a corresponding store/ledger handoff.
- A live process is returned, sent, or task-captured while still provisional.
- Store/ledger custody is removed before termination is confirmed.
- A process entry strongly retains `Session` where only a weak relationship is allowed.

This is the strongest initial consumer.

## 2. `AgentExecutionGuard` and active-turn capacity

A running task stores an `AgentExecutionGuard` in [`RunningTask`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/state/turn.rs:96). Terminal detachment explicitly takes and drops it before opening admission and announcing idle state in [`tasks/mod.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tasks/mod.rs:1372). A regression test requires the execution permit to be released before idle callbacks in [`session/tests.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/session/tests.rs:10992).

**Semantic escape**

The guard is allowed to escape `start_task` into the active-turn object. It must not escape the active-turn domain into terminal receipt tasks, retry work, detached state, or post-idle session state.

**Channels**

- Storage in `RunningTask`
- `Option::take` and move
- Closure/task capture
- Return from startup helpers
- Storage inside another `Arc`-owned object
- Transfer into a terminal coordinator

**Behavioral importance**

Over-retention causes capacity leaks, false busy state, starvation, or deadlock. Releasing too early allows overlapping executions. Releasing after idle notification exposes inconsistent lifecycle state to observers.

**Minimum analysis**

- Treat the guard as a configured linear drop capability.
- Follow moves, `Option::take`, explicit drop, and closure capture.
- Model active-turn removal, interaction release, admission opening, and idle notification as semantic boundaries.
- Ask the existing control-flow/MIR layer for dominance only where exact release-before-boundary proof is required.

**Lexical vs. ownership vs. domain**

- Lexical: correctly sees escape from `start_task`, but that escape is required.
- Ownership: identifies the unique holder and the release operation.
- Lifetime-domain: says retention is valid during the active turn and invalid after interaction release or idle publication.

**Valuable diagnostics**

- Idle/admission boundary reached while the guard remains reachable.
- Guard captured by terminal receipt, retry, or other background work.
- Active turn cleared without a preceding take/drop.
- Terminal or fail-safe path lacks guard release.
- Guard moved into an unknown longer-lived owner.

## 3. Session-owned terminal tasks

The session’s terminal task tracker explicitly owns terminal coordinators so request cancellation cannot strand a claimed turn; see [`session.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/session/session.rs:57). Terminal finalizers are transferred into that tracker in [`tasks/mod.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tasks/mod.rs:1304), and shutdown tests verify that finalization and receipt delivery are drained in [`session/tests.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/session/tests.rs:11013).

**Semantic escape**

Terminal work must escape the initiating request’s cancellation domain, but only into the session-owned tracker whose shutdown protocol waits for it.

This is a case where failure to escape is just as dangerous as escaping to the wrong place.

**Channels**

- Capture by a spawned future
- Tracker-owned spawn versus detached `tokio::spawn`
- Returned future or `JoinHandle`
- Capture of session, turn, terminal authority, or receipt state
- Helper functions that hide the spawn operation

**Behavioral importance**

- Request cancellation can strand a claimed terminal transition.
- Terminal receipts or persistence may be lost.
- Shutdown may complete while required work is still running.
- Detached work can retain session state indefinitely.

**Minimum analysis**

- Join task-capture facts with value provenance.
- Classify spawn sites by owner: request-local, detached, session-tracked, or unknown.
- Summarize wrapper helpers that delegate into `TaskTracker::spawn`.
- Track the transition request/turn → session-shutdown-tracked.
- Recognize tracker close/wait as a terminal boundary.

**Lexical vs. ownership vs. domain**

- Lexical: all spawned captures escape.
- Ownership: distinguishes tracker-owned work from an unowned detached task.
- Lifetime-domain: determines whether request-independent survival is required and whether session shutdown is the correct upper bound.

**Valuable diagnostics**

- Terminal finalizer or receipt work is detached instead of tracker-owned.
- Claimed terminal authority is held only by a request-cancellable future.
- Terminal permit or coordinator captured by an untracked task.
- Tracker closed while paths can still enqueue terminal work.
- Shutdown path fails to drain the owning tracker.

## 4. Pending approval and input waiters

`TurnState` retains keyed `oneshot::Sender` values for approvals, permissions, user input, elicitations, and dynamic tools in [`turn.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/state/turn.rs:513). **Semantic escape**

A sender created for a request intentionally escapes into the originating turn’s keyed registry. It must remain associated with that turn and request key, and it must eventually be resolved or dropped with the correct cancellation ordering.

**Channels**

- Hash-map insertion
- Wrapper storage such as pending-request records
- Storage through `Arc<Mutex<TurnState>>`
- Send/forward through another channel
- Task capture
- Return into session-global state

**Behavioral importance**

- Under-retention closes the receiver and can look like a user denial.
- Over-retention leaves a tool or turn waiting forever.
- Wrong-key or wrong-turn retention routes responses incorrectly.

**Minimum analysis**

- Configure waiter constructors, registrars, removers, resolvers, and clear operations.

- Combine escape facts with cancellation-before-drop ordering.
- Do not attempt general channel protocol inference.

**Lexical vs. ownership vs. domain**

- Lexical: storage outside the request frame is intentional.
- Ownership: the map becomes the sender’s custodian, and sender destruction is observable.
- Lifetime-domain: ties the waiter to the correct turn and requires discharge before turn detachment.
- Ordering adds the final correctness condition: cancellation must become observable before destructive cleanup.

**Valuable diagnostics**

- Pending waiter stored outside the originating turn.
- Turn termination or detachment leaves live waiters.
- Waiters are cleared before cancellation becomes observable.
- Insertion can overwrite an unresolved sender for the same key.
- Resolution sends without removing the matching registry entry.
- Sender is returned or task-captured into an unrelated domain.

- Returned callbacks or trait objects

- Channel send to longer-lived workers

- Stored in session, thread, static, or persistent cache state.

## 6. Cancellation tokens and transfer guards

The guardian review lifecycle creates a child cancellation token and keeps a drop guard armed until the new review session is installed into manager state in [`review_session.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/guardian/review_session.rs:309). **Channels**

- Token cloning or `child_token`
- Task capture
- Storage in manager/subsystem state
- Return from constructors
- Disarming or transferring a `DropGuard`

**Minimum analysis**

The existing cancellation-lineage logic should remain responsible for parent/child authority. A shared escape layer only needs to provide target-aware capture, storage, and ownership facts.

**Valuable diagnostics**

- Parent token captured into child-owned code that can cancel it.
- Fresh unrelated token severs expected shutdown propagation.
- Installation guard disarmed before state ownership is established.
- Child task outlives its owner without a child-token relationship.
- Guard remains armed or retained after successful handoff.

This is a useful consumer, but not the primary justification because `kda` already has substantial specialized cancellation analysis.

## Recommended semantic-core shape

The layer should produce compact facts rather than decide policy:

```text
EscapeFact {
    subject,
    channel,
    ownership_mode,
    source_domain,
    destination_owner,
    destination_domain,
    execution_phase,
    certainty,
    source_site
}
```

Useful channel categories:

- Return
- Field or collection storage
- Static/global storage
- Task or callback capture
- Channel send
- Trait-object/escaping-closure conversion
- Known retaining call
- Opaque call

Useful ownership modes:

- Move/custody transfer
- Strong shared ownership
- Weak observation
- Borrow
- Handle/authority delegation
- Unknown

Domains should be named roles, not only a total numeric hierarchy. `request → turn → session` is roughly ordered, but “session-owned terminal task” and “ordinary session state” have different shutdown semantics even though both are session-long.

The layer should reuse:

- Local moves, clones, borrows, and definitions from place flow
- Origins and helper propagation from value provenance
- Task, callback, and channel classifications from async relationships
- Execution phases and bounded composition from semantic summaries
- MIR facts only when a consumer needs exact path, drop, or suspension reasoning

It should not attempt:

- Heap-graph reconstruction
- General Rust alias analysis
- Reference-count prediction
- Borrow-checker recreation
- Whole-program lifetime inference
- Treating every `Arc::clone` as an escape
- Treating every opaque-call argument as a definite invalid escape

## Adoption recommendation

Make escape summaries a core, reusable fact layer, while keeping escape policies project-specific.

The best first consumers are:

1. Unified-process custody and registration
2. Terminal task tracker ownership
3. Execution-guard release boundaries
4. Pending-waiter cleanup and ownership

Those cases have explicit KD4 lifecycle contracts, high behavioral stakes, and regression-test evidence. `StepContext` is a strong second wave. Cancellation analysis should consume the shared facts later rather than being rewritten around them.

The layer earns its place because current analyses repeatedly rediscover pieces of the same relation while erasing the most important information: destination owner, lifetime domain, and custody mode. A bounded summary joining those existing facts would unlock meaningful checks without drifting into generic compiler escape analysis.

## Recommendation

Add a **MIR-backed, bounded interprocedural abstract-interpretation layer**.

In plainer terms: build one shared engine that follows program state through compiler-lowered control flow, then emits compact behavioral summaries for callers. This combines limited dataflow and interprocedural summaries as one architectural layer.

That would give `kda` the highest long-term leverage. Its central limitation is no longer identifying functions or resolving calls; it is understanding:

> which value or resource is in which state, on which path, at which program phase, when control crosses a call or exits abnormally.

## Candidate assessment

The check estimates below are ranges of distinct useful check families after maturation. They overlap and should not be added together.

### 1. Limited dataflow over MIR — strongest candidate

**Detectable bugs:** validations missing on one path; state changes performed only on success or only on failure; stale values used after mutation; double or missing terminal operations; incorrect `Result`/enum branches; partial updates; resource or lock state across suspension; wrong value sanitized or authorized; implicit-drop and unwind cleanup failures.

**Why calls are insufficient:** a call edge has no abstract store, path condition, alias relation, exit condition, or knowledge of implicit compiler operations.

**MVP:** lower MIR facts into a stable MIR-lite representation with:

- basic blocks and normal, error, panic/unwind, and cancellation/drop exits;
- places, projections, assignments, moves, borrows, calls, switches, drops, and suspension;
- finite path predicates for booleans, `Option`, `Result`, enum discriminants, small constants, and simple intervals;
- definite/maybe state for initialization, ownership tokens, provenance, typestate, and effects;
- per-exit summaries over receiver, parameters, captures, and return values;
- SCC fixed points for recursion.

**Practical stop:** no SMT solving, arbitrary heap-shape analysis, full borrow checking, raw-pointer alias proofs, exhaustive schedule exploration, or complete unsafe/FFI semantics. Cap path disjunctions, loop iterations, projections, dispatch targets, and specializations; widen to explicit unknown.

**Rust interactions:** analyze generics polymorphically and specialize only when a small concrete type changes semantics; use exact trait implementations where resolved and joined contracts otherwise; model closure captures as synthetic parameters; treat async construction, poll, suspension, completion, and cancellation/drop separately; consume expanded MIR while mapping diagnostics through expansion backtraces; require contracts or summaries for external bodies.

**Estimated leverage:** 25–45 useful check families, with perhaps 8–12 high-confidence families in an initial release.

### 2. Generalized interprocedural summaries

**Detectable bugs:** helper-mediated cleanup omissions, hidden blocking or I/O, commit/publish ordering errors, returned aliases, transferred obligations, error suppression, callback effects, and behavior split across wrappers.

**Why calls are insufficient:** resolution identifies the callee but not its parameter-relative behavior, conditions, outcomes, or returned relationships.

**MVP:** one context-insensitive summary per callable, parameterized by receiver/parameters/captures, with may/must facts separated by normal, `Ok`, `Err`, panic, and cancellation exits. Compose them through SCCs.

**Practical stop:** cap recursion, summary size, dispatch candidates, and provenance depth. Do not reopen dependency bodies or construct a whole-program CFG.

**Rust interactions:** generics use symbolic parameters; trait calls join possible implementation summaries; closures expose captures; async summaries distinguish construction from poll and awaited completion; macro-generated bodies require expanded input; external crates need declared or shipped summaries.

**Estimated leverage:** 15–25 families in isolation, but only about 6–12 net-new here because effects and the semantic-summary model already provide much of the structure.

**Verdict:** necessary output of the recommended engine, but not sufficient by itself. Better summaries of weak local facts remain weak.

### 3. General typestate

**Detectable bugs:** calls in the wrong protocol state, use after close/finalize, double initialization or shutdown, missing handshake steps, illegal retry transitions, and operations performed before readiness.

**Why calls are insufficient:** call order must be associated with a particular object and feasible path, not merely with a sequence of resolved callees.

**MVP:** explicit finite-state contracts attached to symbolic places, with guarded transitions, path joins, and parameter-relative call summaries.

**Practical stop:** protocols must be declared or recognized exactly. Avoid automatically inventing state machines from method names or tracking through arbitrary shared heap aliases.

**Rust interactions:** state can generally remain polymorphic over generic payloads; trait methods need a common transition envelope; closure capture determines whether state is borrowed, mutated, or consumed; async requires cancellation transitions; macro and external APIs require MIR visibility or contracts.

**Estimated leverage:** 8–14 families. The existing opt-in typestate implementation means the largest gain would come from placing it on shared dataflow rather than enlarging it independently.

### 4. Ownership obligations

**Detectable bugs:** leaked handles, missing commit/rollback, unreaped children, unjoined tasks, forgotten acknowledgement, double discharge, transferring a resource without transferring its obligation, and failure-path leaks.

**Why calls are insufficient:** resolution does not say which operation creates, transfers, escapes, or discharges an obligation on each exit.

**MVP:** linear abstract tokens with create, borrow, transfer, discharge, escape, and drop operations, tracked per place and summarized per outcome.

**Practical stop:** do not reproduce Rust’s borrow checker or prove arbitrary alias uniqueness. Shared references, raw pointers, interior mutability, and opaque containers become conservative may-alias or escape boundaries.

**Rust interactions:** generic containers need declared ownership-transformer behavior; traits need obligation envelopes; `FnOnce` captures may transfer tokens; async tasks and futures need cancellation/drop accounting; macros need lowered operations; external resource APIs require contracts.

**Estimated leverage:** 8–14 families. It shares most of its machinery with typestate but emphasizes linear transfer and exit accounting.

### 5. Semantic effects

**Detectable bugs:** blocking in async code, hidden I/O while holding a lock, unauthorized mutation, spawning or detaching unexpectedly, non-cancellation-safe behavior, persistence-before-validation, and impure callbacks.

**Why calls are insufficient:** an edge says nothing about effect kind, target, phase, condition, completion, or may/must status.

**MVP:** parameter-relative effect sets with target, phase, condition, outcome, and provenance, propagated through resolved dispatch.

**Practical stop:** do not infer business semantics from names or claim effect absence when bodies are unavailable. Unknown external or dynamic calls must contaminate relevant effect families.

**Rust interactions:** generics are usually effect-polymorphic; trait methods need implementation envelopes; closures carry callback effects; async needs construction/poll/background phases; macro-generated calls need expansion; external crates need curated effect contracts.

**Estimated leverage:** 10–18 total families, but probably only 3–7 net-new because this is already one of `kda`’s strongest semantic layers.

### 6. Value provenance

**Detectable bugs:** untrusted values reaching command, path, SQL, authorization, or unsafe sinks; validation applied to one value while another is used; secret leakage; stale configuration; cross-tenant or cross-identity value mixing; and inconsistent invariant cohorts.

**Why calls are insufficient:** semantic identity cannot establish data dependence between source, transformation, validation, and sink.

**MVP:** MIR def-use provenance with bounded field sensitivity, explicit source/sink/sanitizer contracts, parameter-to-return summaries, and may/must origins.

**Practical stop:** no arbitrary collection-element correlation, general heap points-to analysis, cryptographic equivalence, or deep alias recovery after escape.

**Rust interactions:** generic transformations are usually provenance-transparent unless contracted otherwise; trait calls use transformers or joined summaries; closure captures preserve origin; async stores provenance across suspension; macro expansion should be lowered; external sources and sanitizers need contracts.

**Estimated leverage:** 12–20 families, perhaps 8–15 incremental over the current sparse, flow-insensitive implementation.

### 7. Concurrency relationships

**Detectable bugs:** lock-order cycles, await-while-holding, detached-task leaks, missing join or abort, send-before-register/lost-wakeup protocols, non-atomic read-modify-write patterns, and incorrect cancellation relationships.

**Why calls are insufficient:** concurrency depends on task identity, overlapping lifetimes, shared-resource identity, ordering, and happens-before—not just call reachability.

**MVP:** a bounded event graph for spawn, capture, lock/unlock, send/receive, join, abort, await, and task exit, backed by place and obligation tracking.

**Practical stop:** no exhaustive scheduler exploration, general race detector, relaxed-memory proof system, or hidden synchronization inference. Restrict analysis to recognized primitives and contracts.

**Rust interactions:** generic wrappers retain resource identity where possible; traits require synchronization contracts; closures define task captures; async state is central; macros require lowered events; standard-library and runtime primitives need external contracts.

**Estimated leverage:** 8–15 families. Valuable, but narrower and dependent on the recommended state engine.

### 8. API contracts

**Detectable bugs:** violated preconditions, ignored failure guarantees, incorrect ownership transfer, callback restrictions, invalid resource lifetimes, and representation or persistence invariants.

**Why calls are insufficient:** call identity provides no semantic meaning for application-specific APIs.

**MVP:** exact-identity contracts containing preconditions, per-exit postconditions, effects, typestate transitions, obligation transfer, and provenance transformers.

**Practical stop:** contract important boundaries rather than annotating every function. Validate contract consistency and treat missing contracts as unknown—not pure or safe.

**Rust interactions:** allow type and const placeholders for generics; place envelopes on trait methods; represent callback contracts for closures; distinguish async phases; bind macro wrappers to expanded identities where stable; use contracts heavily for external crates.

**Estimated leverage:** 8–15 families. `kda` is already contract-oriented, so this would improve precision more than general understanding unless backed by shared dataflow.

### 9. Trait reasoning

**Detectable bugs:** implementations violating semantic trait guarantees, default-method drift, effectful implementations behind supposedly pure interfaces, blanket-implementation holes, and incorrect guarantees through `dyn Trait`.

**Why calls are insufficient:** resolving one call site does not prove that every permitted implementation satisfies a behavioral relation.

**MVP:** semantic envelopes on trait methods, implementation conformance checks, and conservative joins over closed target sets.

**Practical stop:** rely on rustc or rust-analyzer for type and implementation selection. Do not build a second trait solver or assume a public trait’s implementation set is closed.

**Rust interactions:** generics and associated types are fundamental; closures participate through `Fn*` traits; async trait lowering must be understood through generated futures or expansion; macro-generated impls require compiler-lowered identities; external impl sets remain open unless sealed or contracted.

**Estimated leverage:** 6–12 families. Important for scaling another analysis, but too specialized to be the primary semantic core.

### 10. Change-aware analysis

**Detectable bugs:** regressions in effects, failure behavior, provenance, obligations, cleanup, protocol guarantees, and implementation coverage.

**Why calls are insufficient:** a single-revision graph cannot compare behavioral relationships or decide which semantic guarantee changed.

**MVP:** stable callable alignment, semantic-summary comparison, dependency invalidation, and explicit unknown results when revisions or build profiles are incomparable.

**Practical stop:** compare the same target/profile/features; do not attempt general semantic equivalence or confidently align unstable generated bodies.

**Rust interactions:** compare generic and trait envelopes rather than every monomorphization; closures need stable enclosing identities; async requires phase-aware summaries; macro changes need expansion-aware mapping; dependency-version behavior requires external summaries.

**Estimated leverage:** 8–15 regression families. The repository already has substantial change-semantic infrastructure, so its leverage is downstream of better single-revision semantics.

## Proposed architectural shape

The new layer should look like this:

```text
rustc MIR facts
    ↓
stable MIR-lite control-flow and place model
    ↓
bounded abstract interpreter
    ├─ path predicates
    ├─ provenance
    ├─ typestate and obligations
    ├─ effects and ordering
    └─ async/drop/concurrency events
    ↓
per-outcome callable summaries
    ↓
SCC composition through resolved dispatch
    ↓
lint, query, and semantic-diff consumers
```

The existing `SemanticSummary` vocabulary is a good candidate for the boundary product. The important change is that its facts should be produced by one state engine, rather than assembled after several analyses with different notions of path, place, escape, and unknown.

## Why this wins

This layer has the broadest leverage because it strengthens almost every capability already present:

- Effects gain conditions, targets, and per-exit certainty.
- Typestate gains instance identity and real path sensitivity.
- Obligations gain moves, implicit drops, unwind, and cancellation.
- Provenance becomes flow-sensitive.
- Concurrency gains task/resource lifetime relationships.
- API and trait contracts become executable abstract transformers.
- Change analysis compares substantially stronger guarantees.
- Existing semantic identity and call resolution become the interprocedural skeleton rather than the endpoint.

The right practical target is not “understand every Rust program completely.” Unsafe code, FFI, open-world dynamic dispatch, missing dependency bodies, and unrestricted heap aliasing make that impossible. The target should be:

> precise on compiler-visible local state and declared boundaries, conservative everywhere else, with may/must/unknown preserved explicitly.

That is the architectural addition most likely to turn `kda` from a broad collection of semantic detectors into a general Rust reasoning platform. No files were changed.

# Recommended reusable object model

A reusable analyzer object should contain:

1. **Contract**
   - stable protocol ID;
   - exact semantic generators;
   - subject/key recipe;
   - required predicate;
   - accepted exits and observation horizon;
   - transfer/remapping rules;
   - relevant unknown capabilities.

2. **Instance**
   - creation span and callable;
   - semantic identity;
   - current owner;
   - domain state;
   - causal parent instances.

3. **Evidence**
   - resolved semantic operation;
   - subject/key;
   - outcome and execution phase;
   - completion strength: attempted, completed, durable, externally observed;
   - must/may classification;
   - ordering edges.

4. **Proof result**
   - `Proved` with the witnesses that discharged it;
   - `Violated` with a concrete counterexample path;
   - `Unknown` with the exact boundary or exhausted bound;
   - violations should continue to take precedence when another path is merely unknown.

5. **Pluggable domain**
   - finite protocol/typestate;
   - keyed reachability;
   - relational predicate or set inclusion;
   - monotonic lattice;
   - affine/cardinality;
   - ordered effect correspondence.

Individual lints would then become obligation generators and diagnostic projections. Several diagnostics could inspect the same proven or outstanding instance without repeating call resolution, alias approximation, CFG traversal, or uncertainty handling.

## Conclusion

Yes. `cargo kda` could analyze meaningful classes of generic instantiations without enumerating concrete types.

The right question is not “which monomorphizations exist?” but:

> Under this function’s parameter environment, which semantic type/implementation families are possible, and does any family violate a declared invariant?

Conceptually:

```text
parameter bounds
∧ associated-type constraints
∧ applicable impl-family constraints
∧ relevant semantic properties
∧ negated project contract
```

A satisfiable result yields a counterexample class; a disproven result establishes the contract; ambiguity becomes `Unknown`.

This matches how Rust reasons about generic bodies: trait goals are evaluated in a `ParamEnv` containing the applicable bounds and where-clauses. Monomorphization merely chooses an already-permitted instance; it is rarely the underlying correctness problem. [Rust compiler guide: parameter environments](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/typing-parameter-envs.html)

## Where valid generic code goes wrong

| Area | Type-correct but semantically dangerous assumption |
|---|---|
| Trait bounds | `T: Add`, `From`, or `Iterator` is treated as a behavioral law rather than permission to call methods. |
| Associated types | `<T as Trait>::Item` is assumed non-ZST, trivially dropped, `Send`, or representation-compatible without such a constraint. |
| Specialization-like behavior | A default method, concrete impl, blanket impl, marker dispatch, or `TypeId` branch has behavior inconsistent with other families. |
| ZSTs | `Sized` is mistaken for nonzero size; allocation and pointer iteration skip elements or mishandle capacity. Rust explicitly permits zero-sized `Sized` types. [Rust type layout](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/reference/type-layout.html) |

## 1. Constraint-based conditional summaries

Summarize a generic body once, but make the summary conditional on properties of its parameters and projections:

```text
size(T) = zero        ⇒ use ZST allocation/iteration path
needs_drop(T)         ⇒ may activate T's drop effects
Send(T) ∧ Send(U)     ⇒ Send(Wrapper<T, U>)       // when compiler-proved
I: FusedIterator      ⇒ None is terminal
callback_calls(F)     = [1, 3]                    // path-derived
<T as Source>::Item: Send
```

The summary should contain only properties actually demanded by the body or its contract.

- Bug classes caught: ZST-sensitive containers, conditional `Drop` effects, associated-type leaks, callback cardinality, numeric boundary conditions, and generic wrapper preservation.
- Why type checking is insufficient: the body remains well-formed for every permitted `T`; the failure is that a contract requires a stronger property than its bounds express.
- Explosion control: use a demand-driven finite lattice. Do not track numeric width unless arithmetic or casts matter; do not split on `Drop` unless destruction matters. Join equivalent paths, cap clauses/depth, and widen overflow to `Unknown`.
- External crates: compiler-proved predicates such as normalized associated types and auto traits are usable. An external method’s behavioral effects remain unknown unless provided by a versioned contract or imported summary.

This is a natural extension of KDA’s existing conditional and parameter-relative summaries. Its current policy already requires bounded domains and turns overflow or ambiguous targets into unknown rather than absence: [bounded-cross-function-reasoning.md](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/docs/bounded-cross-function-reasoning.md:84).

## 2. Trait-family summaries

Treat each authored impl header as a family, rather than enumerating all types satisfying it:

```rust
impl<T: FastPath> Encode for Wrapper<T> { ... }
impl Encode for Concrete { ... }
```

The family identity consists of the impl definition, its predicates, associated-type bindings, and whether a method is overridden or inherited from the trait default. Stable Rust need not support general specialization for this to matter: defaults, disjoint impls, marker traits, newtypes, and runtime type discrimination already create specialization-like behavior.

- Bug classes caught: one impl omits a required effect, inherits an unsuitable default, selects a different associated representation, performs a lossy conversion, invokes callbacks differently, or changes error propagation.
- Why type checking is insufficient: Rust checks signatures, required items, coherence, and overlap. It does not require all impls to satisfy an unwritten semantic role. Traits may redefine default items, while coherence only prevents overlapping applicable impls. [Rust implementation rules](https://doc.rust-lang.org/reference/items/implementations.html)
- Explosion control: produce one summary per impl definition, not per implementing type. Group impl families with identical effective summaries and solve only the applicability predicates relevant to an observed divergence.
- External crates: distinguish three states:
  - closed for the current locked compilation;
  - downstream-open, particularly for a public locally defined trait;
  - opaque dependency behavior.

  Must-properties require a sealed/closed family or explicit external contracts. An unknown downstream impl must block a universal proof.

KDA already has much of the scaffolding: [`trait_behavior_matrix.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/trait_behavior_matrix.rs:38) distinguishes overrides from inherited defaults and marks public traits as potentially open to downstream implementations. The missing part is symbolic impl applicability, associated-type equations, and parameter-conditioned summaries.

- Layout: `ZST | non-ZST | DST | unknown`
- Destruction: `trivial | transitive | custom/effectful | unknown`
- Auto traits: `proven | disproven | conditional | unknown` for `Send` and `Sync`
- Numeric: signedness, width class, primitive/custom operator, checked/wrapping/saturating/unknown
- Iterator: fused/non-fused, exact/inexact, finite/unbounded/unknown
- Conversion: identity, project-proven round-trip, fallible, lossy, unknown
- Callback: `FnOnce/FnMut/Fn`, invocation interval, phase, reentrancy/effects
- Wrapper relation: preserves, masks, amplifies, delays, or conditionally forwards an inner property

# Design thesis

`cargo kda` should become a semantic evidence engine and bounded proof system for Rust—not a second compiler and not a growing collection of unrelated lints.

Its core pipeline should be:

```text
program worlds
    → authoritative language facts
    → typed semantic relations
    → compositional behavioral summaries
    → contract proofs and exploratory queries
    → diagnostics or revision deltas
```

A “program” is not merely a repository checkout. Every result belongs to a semantic world:

```text
source revision
+ Cargo dependency resolution
+ crate instance
+ target triple and target features
+ enabled Cargo features and cfg values
+ edition and rustc version
+ profile/panic strategy
+ build-script and proc-macro outputs
+ KDA contract set
```

The same source file may represent different HIR under different crate instances or `cfg` sets. rust-analyzer explicitly models HIR in a particular crate/configuration context, rather than attaching a single meaning to source text. [rust-analyzer architecture](https://rust-analyzer.github.io/book/contributing/architecture.html)

This world identity must qualify every persistent KDA fact.

## Necessary semantic layers

| Layer | Meaning | Primary source |
|---|---|---|
| World and revision | Which program configuration is being discussed | Cargo, toolchain, VFS, KDA configuration |
| Semantic identity | Definitions, implementations, callable instances, places, expansions | rust-analyzer; rustc identities for MIR |
| Type and trait structure | Types, substitutions, bounds, normalization, impl selection, coercions | rust-analyzer for interactive use; rustc when authoritative |
| Executable structure | CFG, calls, drops, unwinding, coroutine suspension | rustc MIR where available; bounded RA lowering otherwise |
| Value and ownership flow | Origin, move, borrow, capture, return, escape, semantic responsibility | MIR plus KDA abstraction |
| Behavioral effects | State mutation, I/O, persistence, spawn, block, emit, terminate | KDA inference plus reviewed contracts |
| Temporal behavior | Outcomes, ordering, phases, state transitions, cancellation | KDA summaries |
| Obligations | Preconditions and responsibilities created, transferred, or discharged | Contracts plus derived facts |
| Concurrency | Tasks, threads, locks, channels, atomics, happens-before relations | MIR/RA facts plus runtime-library contracts |
| Unsafe reasoning | Pointer validity, provenance, aliasing, layout, FFI, unsafe-trait assumptions | rustc facts, explicit safety contracts, evidence |
| Change semantics | Which facts and guarantees changed between compatible worlds | Dual semantic snapshots |
| Presentation | Failing findings, exploratory answers, patch review | Separate consumers of the same fact model |

The layers must share one identity system, evidence model, and uncertainty discipline. They must not independently rediscover functions, calls, places, or effects.

## 1. What rustc owns and KDA should never duplicate

“Owns” means authoritative for compilation semantics, even where KDA obtains a convenient approximation from rust-analyzer.

KDA should never implement its own competing version of:

- Parsing validity and macro hygiene.
- Name resolution and privacy.
- Expression and pattern type checking.
- Generic substitution and type normalization.
- Trait solving, coherence, impl overlap, and associated-item resolution.
- Lifetime checking, borrow checking, moves, partial initialization, and compiler drop checking.
- Pattern exhaustiveness.
- Const evaluation and const-validity rules.
- Layout, ABI, niches, discriminants, and target-specific representation.
- Exact MIR construction, coroutine lowering, drop elaboration, or unwind edges.
- Compiler acceptance, compiler diagnostics, or Clippy’s ordinary lint space.
- Codegen instance selection and monomorphization semantics.

rustc lowers typed HIR through THIR into MIR, using MIR for flow-sensitive checks including borrow checking and later code generation. [rustc compiler overview](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/overview.html) MIR is therefore the right authority for moves, actual control-flow lowering, drops, cleanup blocks, and coroutine structure. [rustc MIR guide](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/mir/index.html)

KDA may normalize compiler facts into a stable schema, but it must preserve their meaning and provenance. It should not replace a missing compiler fact with a name-based guess and then present that guess as proof.

The existing versioned MIR-export boundary is the correct architectural direction: isolate unstable `rustc_private` integration in a toolchain-matched producer and expose a stable fact protocol to the analyzer. See [docs/mir-fact-protocol.md](C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/docs/mir-fact-protocol.md:1).

Rustc does **not** own repository intent or all correctness. Safe Rust can violate logical constraints that neither compile-time nor runtime checking enforces—for example, coherence between `Eq` and `Hash`. [Rust Reference: logic errors](https://doc.rust-lang.org/reference/behavior-not-considered-unsafe.html) This space is KDA’s reason to exist.

## 2. What rust-analyzer provides as raw semantic infrastructure

rust-analyzer should be KDA’s responsive source-semantic frontend. It provides:

- Cargo project loading, crate graphs, dependency relationships, targets, features, and `cfg` context.
- A VFS and immutable incremental analysis snapshots.
- Lossless syntax and authored source coordinates.
- Macro expansion, hygiene, and source-to-expansion mapping.
- HIR definitions for modules, functions, traits, impls, ADTs, variants, fields, aliases, constants, closures, and async blocks.
- Resolved paths, references, calls, and method calls.
- Expression and pattern types.
- Function signatures, generics, bounds, associated items, and trait relationships.
- Source-to-HIR and HIR-to-source navigation.
- Reference search and enough IDE infrastructure to locate semantic owners and affected source.

rust-analyzer describes its `hir` facade as a static, fully resolved view, backed by an incremental database; its syntax trees deliberately contain no semantic information. [rust-analyzer architecture](https://rust-analyzer.github.io/book/contributing/architecture.html)

KDA should treat this as raw infrastructure, not as its reasoning model. In particular, rust-analyzer does not provide:

- An authoritative borrow-check result suitable for proving KDA findings.
- A complete runtime call graph.
- All monomorphized callable instances.
- Complete dynamic-dispatch, callback, FFI, or dependency-body behavior.
- A repository effect system.
- Business invariants, protocol obligations, or semantic change review.
- Complete unsafe-code soundness reasoning.

The current shared [AnalysisContext](C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/analysis_context.rs:481) is the right integration point. It should evolve into a semantic database facade rather than remaining a cache of several partially overlapping indexes.

## 3. What reasoning layer KDA should add

### A. Identity

KDA needs distinct identities for distinct semantic concepts:

- `WorldId`: complete build/configuration world.
- `CrateInstanceId`: package plus dependency/configuration instance.
- `DefinitionId`: nominal definition in that crate instance.
- `CallableInstanceId`: definition plus relevant substitutions and parameter environment.
- `ImplId`: trait/inherent implementation, including its predicates.
- `BodyId`: function, closure, async block, generator, drop glue, or shim.
- `PlaceId`: local/static/parameter plus typed projections.
- `ResourceId`: semantic resource or capability, often symbolic.
- `ProtocolSubjectId`: value, key, aggregate, or external entity whose state is tracked.
- `ContractId`: reviewed non-language rule.

A definition and an instantiation must not be conflated. `T::method` in generic code is not necessarily one executable target. A source location is evidence, not identity. A rename matcher may suggest cross-revision continuity, but it must be labeled probable rather than silently rewriting identity.

### B. Type and trait relationship graph

KDA should preserve relationships that explain risk:

- Nominal type structure, variants, fields, visibility, generics, and `repr`.
- References, raw pointers, mutability, lifetimes where semantically relevant, and pinning.
- Type aliases, associated types, opaque `impl Trait`, and normalization context.
- Trait implementations, blanket impls, negative impls, supertraits, and default methods.
- Effective method body: implementation override versus trait default.
- Coercions, autoderef/autoref, unsizing, closure captures, and function-item coercion.
- Auto-trait outcomes such as `Send`, `Sync`, `Unpin`, and unwind-safety traits.
- `Drop`, `Deref`, conversion, indexing, operator, and `FromResidual` relationships that hide behavior behind syntax.

Trait coherence and orphan rules are compiler facts, but the consequences of adding an impl or changing a bound are review facts. [Rust Reference: trait coherence](https://doc.rust-lang.org/reference/items/implementations.html)

### C. Call and activation relations

A call edge needs more than caller and callee:

```text
caller
→ dispatch form
→ possible target set
→ argument/place substitution
→ path predicate
→ execution phase
→ completion relationship
```

Dispatch forms include direct calls, resolved trait calls, generic trait obligations, trait objects, function pointers, closures, registered callbacks, drop glue, overloaded operators, async polling, and FFI.

Execution phases should distinguish:

- Immediate execution.
- Future construction.
- Poll-time execution.
- Awaited completion.
- Enqueued work.
- Detached/background work.
- Callback execution.
- Destructor execution.
- Cleanup during unwind or cancellation.

Unknown targets remain explicit open boundaries. A closed candidate set permits stronger conclusions; an open candidate set never proves absence.

### D. Control-flow summaries

Use MIR for authoritative bodies and a clearly labeled source-HIR fallback for incomplete editing scenarios.

Local summaries should retain:

- Normal, `Ok`, `Err`, panic/unwind, abort, divergence, pending, and cancellation/drop exits.
- Must- and may-execute operations.
- Dominance, post-dominance, and relevant ordering.
- Cleanup on every applicable exit.
- Path predicates in deliberately small domains.
- Loop widening and loss of precision.
- Phase changes across async/spawn/callback boundaries.

Rust destruction is behavior: assignment may drop an old value, partial moves affect which fields drop, and trait objects invoke the concrete destructor. [Rust Reference: destructors](https://doc.rust-lang.org/reference/destructors.html) Drop effects must therefore participate in call, effect, obligation, and change analysis.

The existing [SemanticSummary](C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/semantic_summary.rs:504)—outcome-indexed effects, flows, obligations, ordering, and explicit unknowns—is the closest current component to this kernel.

### E. Value, ownership, and provenance

KDA must distinguish four concepts currently liable to be called “provenance”:

1. **Evidence provenance:** which compiler fact, source span, contract, or derivation supports a claim.
2. **Value lineage:** where data came from and through which transformations it passed.
3. **Semantic ownership:** who is responsible for completing, cancelling, freeing, publishing, or acknowledging something.
4. **Pointer provenance:** which allocation and derivation authorize a raw-pointer access.

Rustc owns language-level moves and borrow validity. KDA adds semantic responsibility:

- Move, borrow, clone/share, capture, return, retain, escape, transfer, and destroy.
- Ownership of handles, permits, registrations, transactions, callbacks, raw resources, and background work.
- Whether `Drop` discharges a responsibility.
- Whether `Arc`-style sharing duplicates access while preserving one underlying resource identity.
- Whether `mem::forget`, `ManuallyDrop`, FFI, or detachment defeats normal cleanup.

### F. Effects

Effects are not Rust language facts. They should be an extensible semantic ontology, initially covering:

- State reads and mutations.
- Resource acquisition, transfer, release, and escape.
- Persistence reads, writes, commits, and durability barriers.
- External I/O.
- Event emission.
- Blocking and suspension.
- Spawn, join, abort, and detach.
- Cancellation checks and cancellation-sensitive work.
- Termination, panic, and process effects.
- Unsafe operations and FFI crossings.
- Repository-defined domain operations.

Each fact needs target, phase, outcome, predicate, must/may certainty, evidence, and completeness scope.

External functions receive reviewed contracts or remain unknown. Name patterns may nominate contracts but should not silently establish them.

Interprocedural inference should use monotone, bounded SCC fixed points:

- Sequence unions effects.
- Alternative control flow unions `may` and intersects `must`.
- Argument substitution remaps symbolic places.
- Unknown boundaries taint only the capability families they could affect.
- Widening loses precision monotonically.

### G. Typestate and obligations

Typestate is a finite abstraction over a semantic subject:

```text
subject × current-state × event → possible next states
```

Subjects may be values, keyed resources, fields, registrations, transactions, actors, or persistent records. State evidence can come from enum variants, type transitions, predicates, fields, or declared operations.

Obligations are separate from states. They express responsibility:

```text
create → preserve | transfer | discharge | permitted abandonment
```

Every obligation should identify:

- Its subject and semantic owner.
- Preconditions for using an operation.
- The event that created it.
- Allowed transfers.
- Required terminal conditions by outcome and phase.
- Whether `Drop`, cancellation, unwind, or process exit discharges it.
- The witness path proving violation or incompleteness.

This is how KDA answers “what non-obvious obligation does this code create?” without pretending that every protocol can be inferred.

### H. Concurrency

KDA should model a bounded event graph:

- Threads, tasks, processes, callbacks, and executors.
- Spawn ownership: structured, joined, abortable, detached, or unknown.
- Locks and lock ordering.
- Channels and send/receive closure.
- Atomic events and declared ordering requirements.
- Suspension while holding resources.
- Cancellation and cleanup edges.
- Known happens-before relations from join, mutex, channel, and explicit protocol contracts.

Safe Rust prevents ordinary data races, but it does not prevent deadlocks, logical races, stale publication, lost updates, detached-task leaks, or incorrect atomic protocols. `Send` and `Sync` are especially important unsafe obligations because unsafe code may rely on their implementations. [Rustonomicon: Send and Sync](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nomicon/send-and-sync.html)

### I. Unsafe contract reasoning

Every unsafe boundary should have a structured contract:

```text
required assumptions
→ unsafe operation
→ guarantees established
→ obligations transferred to callers or retained internally
```

Relevant assumption families include:

- Non-nullness, alignment, initialization, and type validity.
- Allocation membership, bounds, and pointer derivation.
- Aliasing and exclusivity.
- Lifetime and deallocation ordering.
- Layout, ABI, discriminants, metadata, and vtables.
- Pinning and address stability.
- Thread confinement, atomics, and synchronization.
- FFI ownership, callbacks, unwinding, and allocator compatibility.
- Requirements of unsafe traits and unsafe attributes.

An `unsafe` block does not weaken Rust’s validity rules; it makes the programmer responsible for satisfying them. Rust also does not yet have one complete formal unsafe-code model, particularly around aliasing and provenance. [Rust Reference: undefined behavior](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/reference/behavior-considered-undefined.html)

Consequently, KDA can verify particular contracts and expose missing evidence, but it must not claim general soundness.

### J. One truth and evidence lattice

Every relation should carry:

- Semantic world.
- Typed subject, predicate, and object.
- `must`, `may`, `conditional`, or `unknown` certainty.
- `exact`, `bounded`, `open-world`, or `heuristic` precision.
- Path/configuration predicates.
- Source and expansion witnesses.
- Derivation chain.
- Dependencies used for invalidation.
- A completeness declaration for the relation family.

Negative conclusions require a closed search domain. Heuristic evidence may support interactive exploration or contract discovery, but never a failing correctness diagnostic by itself.

## 4. What belongs in diagnostics

Diagnostics should report proven violations of language-external contracts or narrowly defined high-confidence policies.

A diagnostic should contain:

- Stable rule and finding identity.
- The violated contract or invariant.
- The relevant semantic world.
- Primary authored location and minimal secondary locations.
- Actual versus required semantic relationship.
- A counterexample path or obligation chain.
- Outcome, phase, and configuration under which it occurs.
- Certainty, precision, and relevant unknown boundaries.
- Concrete remediation or the decision that must be documented.
- A stable fingerprint for baselining.

Good examples include:

- An obligation provably remains live on an error or cancellation exit.
- A safe wrapper reaches an unsafe operation without establishing a declared precondition.
- A lock-order cycle is proven through helper summaries.
- A trait implementation violates a declared behavioral envelope.
- An enum variant enters persistence without a corresponding decode or migration projection.
- A patch removes cleanup that previously post-dominated resource creation.

Diagnostics should not contain:

- Ordinary rustc or Clippy findings.
- Speculative whole-program conclusions.
- Long exploratory call graphs.
- “Could not prove safe” presented as “unsafe.”
- Invariant guesses based only on naming or repetition.

Uncertainty may have its own advisory diagnostic class, but it must be phrased as missing review evidence, not a correctness failure.

## 5. What belongs in interactive queries

Queries should expose more information and tolerate incompleteness:

- What effects can this callable transitively cause?
- Which effects occur before return, on await, in the background, or during drop?
- Which callers can reach this operation?
- Which target implementations may receive this trait call?
- Where did this value, capability, or authority originate?
- Who currently owns an obligation and how can it terminate?
- Which state transitions are possible from this point?
- What unsafe assumptions reach this operation?
- Why is this fact believed?
- What blocks KDA from answering exactly?
- Under which target or feature configuration does the answer change?

The current large [QueryKind](C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/semantic_query/model.rs:5) enum should eventually become a set of views over a smaller relation algebra:

```text
resolve entity
→ select relation families
→ traverse forward or backward
→ filter by world/predicate/phase/outcome
→ summarize
→ explain derivation
```

Natural-language questions can compile into that algebra. The structured result—not generated prose—remains the source of truth.

## 6. What belongs in change review

A patch is only a seed for analysis. KDA should build compatible semantic snapshots of both revisions and compare normalized facts.

The semantic surface includes:

- Public and crate-visible API shape.
- Generic bounds, associated types, trait impls, auto traits, object safety, and dispatch targets.
- Type representation, enum variants, discriminants, serialization, wire, and persistence projections.
- Call reachability and dependency boundaries.
- Effects, phases, completion behavior, and failure fate.
- State machines and possible transitions.
- Created, transferred, strengthened, weakened, or abandoned obligations.
- Ownership and escape behavior.
- Drop, unwind, and cancellation behavior.
- Unsafe preconditions and guarantees.
- Concurrency topology and happens-before assumptions.
- Configuration-specific behavior.

Deltas should be classified as:

- Added or removed fact.
- Strengthened or weakened guarantee.
- Expanded or narrowed target set.
- New or closed unknown boundary.
- Changed predicate/configuration scope.
- Changed ownership or obligation owner.
- Changed ordering, outcome, or execution phase.
- Semantically equivalent despite textual change.

Impact analysis then follows typed reverse dependencies: callers, trait users, implementors, constructors, consumers, protocol participants, unsafe dependents, tests, and public boundaries. Risk is determined by the kind of relationship changed, its reach, and uncertainty—not line count.

Old and new snapshots are comparable only when their toolchain, target, features, dependencies, generated inputs, and contract schemas are compatible. Otherwise the review must expose a cohort mismatch.

The repository currently has duplicate semantic-family models in [semantic_diff.rs](C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/semantic_diff.rs:18) and [semantic_revision.rs](C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/semantic_revision.rs:41); these should converge on one revision schema.

## 7. What is too expensive or theoretically difficult

KDA should explicitly decline general solutions to:

- Exact whole-program call graphs in the presence of open trait worlds, function pointers, callbacks, FFI, plugins, and dynamic loading.
- Complete monomorphization of every possible downstream instantiation.
- Exact heap aliasing or raw-pointer provenance.
- General proof of unsafe-code soundness.
- Arbitrary invariant or programmer-intent inference.
- Full path-sensitive interprocedural symbolic execution.
- Behavioral equivalence between arbitrary revisions.
- Complete deadlock, liveness, or all-schedule concurrency verification.
- Exact performance, timing, resource consumption, or scheduler behavior.
- Universal downstream semver compatibility.
- Precise behavior of arbitrary external code, inline assembly, build scripts, proc macros, or foreign runtimes.

Several are undecidable in general; others have prohibitive state spaces or depend on unavailable environments.

Practical substitutes are bounded abstract interpretation, reviewed contracts, closed-world modes, targeted model checking, Miri and sanitizers as dynamic evidence, and explicit `Unknown`. Limits must be deterministic and semantic—depth, fact count, candidate count, or fixed-point widening—not wall-clock time.

# Staged architecture

1. **Consolidate the current semantic kernel.** Freeze the creation of new standalone semantic models. Make `AnalysisContext`, stable identities, evidence, uncertainty, and the callable `SemanticSummary` the common substrate. 2. **Make worlds and identities authoritative.** Introduce full `WorldId`, crate-instance, definition, callable-instance, place, resource, and contract identities. Persist source/expansion witnesses separately. Add compatibility rules and exact/probable/unmatched cross-revision identity matching.

4. **Build one compositional summary engine.** Make call relations, value flow, ownership, effects, outcomes, phases, ordering, and unknown boundaries first-class summary domains. Solve SCCs monotonically with deterministic caps. Add external and trait contracts without embedding repository-specific API names in the engine.

5. **Add protocol proof domains.** Layer finite typestate, keyed identity, ownership transfer, terminal obligations, unsafe preconditions, and bounded concurrency reasoning over the shared summaries. Require explicit contracts for failing findings; invariant discovery only nominates contracts for review.

6. **Replace query proliferation with a semantic query algebra.** Support entity resolution, typed traversal, filtering, summarization, and derivation explanation. Preserve structured results for agents and render focused human answers from them.

7. **Unify revision and patch review.** Store versioned semantic snapshots with coverage and invalidation dependencies. Compare old and new worlds, classify relationship deltas, propagate impact, and produce a review organized around changed guarantees, obligations, dispatch, unsafe assumptions, and uncertainty.

8. **Generalize and harden.** Validate against diverse Rust repositories, targets, feature matrices, async runtimes, FFI styles, and unsafe patterns. Publish reusable ecosystem contract packs separately from the engine. Measure precision, unknown-boundary rate, stability across harmless edits, incremental cost, and diagnostic false-positive rate.

The end state is not “a smarter lint runner.” It is a configuration-aware semantic graph, a bounded abstract interpreter, and a contract proof system whose diagnostics, queries, and patch reviews are three views of the same evidence.

# Conclusion

Yes. Capability-flow reasoning would let `cargo kda` verify important properties that an ordinary call graph cannot.

The decisive distinction is:

- A call graph asks whether code can reach an effectful function.
- Capability flow asks which authority-bearing value reaches that function, where it came from, what subject it authorizes, whether it was widened or substituted, and whether it crossed a forbidden ownership or lifecycle boundary.

KD4 contains several strong candidates. Some are explicit capability objects; others are clonable contexts, handles, or RAII witnesses that function as capabilities despite not being named that way.

This was a read-only investigation; I changed no files.

## Candidate capabilities

### 1. `ToolInvocation`: authorized tool-dispatch envelope

[`ToolInvocation`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/context.rs:76) bundles the session, turn context, cancellation token, call identity, tool name, source, payload, tracker, and step context. Possessing it is effectively permission to invoke a tool against a particular session and turn.

- **Origin:** The tool router constructs it only after deferred-tool activation, review authorization, and typed-tool authorization checks in [`router.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/router.rs:342).

- **Propagation:** It is passed through the registry, cloned at the dynamic `CoreToolRuntime` boundary, then consumed by `ToolExecutor<ToolInvocation>` implementations in [`registry.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/registry.rs:1268). Handlers commonly destructure it and pass its session or turn-derived components onward.

- **Expected boundary:** Only the authorized router path should mint one. A handler should receive an invocation whose session, step, tool name, payload, and call identity all belong to the authorization decision that preceded dispatch.

- **Compiling misuse:** Code inside `codex-core` can construct a `ToolInvocation` and invoke a concrete handler directly, bypassing router authorization. Its fields are sufficiently accessible within the crate, and test code already constructs invocations directly. A caller can also rebuild one by combining a session from one operation with a payload or turn context from another.

- **Required representation:** A branded capability such as
  `AuthorizedInvocation(session, step, call_id, tool_name, payload_subject)`, with a mint-source contract on the router and preservation contracts for registry and trait dispatch.

- **Beyond security:** The same rule verifies causal correlation: logs, events, cancellation, and results must remain attached to the originating step and call.

A call graph sees the same handler edge for a legitimate routed invocation and a hand-constructed one. It cannot distinguish them.

### 2. `SandboxAttempt`: permission to perform an external execution

[`SandboxAttempt`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/sandboxing.rs:429) contains the effective sandbox type, permission profiles, managed-network enforcement, writable roots, working directory, sandbox manager, and network proxy. [`ToolRuntime::run`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/sandboxing.rs:412) accepts it as the execution authority.

Related traits make this a protocol:

- [`Approvable`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/sandboxing.rs:331) defines approval subjects and requirements.
- `Sandboxable` derives sandbox preferences.
- `ToolCtx` carries session, turn, call, and tool identity.
- `ToolRuntime` converts the authorized attempt into an external effect.

- **Origin:** [`ToolOrchestrator::run`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/orchestrator.rs:145) derives approval requirements, obtains or rejects approval, computes the sandbox policy, and constructs the initial attempt from the current turn policy.

- **Propagation:** `run_attempt` may augment it with an execution-specific network proxy and then passes it through the `ToolRuntime` trait to shell or unified-exec runtimes.

- **Expected boundary:** The attempt should be no more permissive than the current policy and approval. The request approved must be the request executed. Direct runtime invocation should not bypass the orchestrator.

- **Compiling misuse:** Crate-internal code can construct a `SandboxAttempt` with `SandboxType::None`, permissive filesystem settings, or disabled managed-network enforcement, then call a runtime directly. Another wrapper could approve request A but execute request B with the approved attempt.

- **Required representation:** Per-field authority lattices plus relations:

  - `attempt_authority ≤ policy_authority`
  - `attempt_authority ≤ approved_authority`
  - `approved_subject == executed_subject`
  - approval dominates execution on every path
  - only approved mint sites may create execution-ready attempts

- **Beyond security:** This generalizes to configuration lineage: effective runtime configuration must be derived from the authoritative turn snapshot rather than a stale or unrelated configuration.

Kda’s existing [`typed_authority.rs`](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/typed_authority.rs:1) is already a narrow prototype of this analysis.

### 3. Active and deferred network approvals: scoped lease capabilities

[`ActiveNetworkApproval`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/network_approval.rs:90) owns an approval registration, cancellation state, mode, and execution proxy. It can be transformed into [`DeferredNetworkApproval`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/tools/network_approval.rs:59), which keeps the proxy alive for a continuing process.

- **Origin:** `begin_network_approval` registers the approval against a session service and, on Linux, derives an execution proxy for an exact environment and attribution token.

- **Propagation:** The active proxy is inserted into `SandboxAttempt`. Long-running unified-exec processes retain a cloneable deferred approval until process completion.

- **Expected boundary:** The proxy and registration should remain bound to their original session, environment, request, and process. Active approval should transition to either finished or deferred; deferred approval should finish only when its associated process finishes.

- **Compiling misuse:** A deferred approval can be attached to the wrong process or completed through a separately supplied service. Its type does not brand it with the originating session or process. Similarly, internal code can place an execution proxy into an unrelated attempt.

- **Required representation:** A subject tuple such as
  `(session, turn, registration, environment, request_or_process)`, plus typestate `Active → Deferred → Finished`. Because deferred approvals are deliberately cloneable and internally coordinated, this is a shared lease rather than a simple linear token.

- **Beyond security:** It is also a resource-lifetime rule: background process lifetime, proxy lifetime, cancellation, and completion must agree.

### 4. Selected capability roots: access to environment-backed filesystems

[`SelectedCapabilityRoot`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/protocol/src/capabilities.rs:8) is a stable, serializable descriptor containing an environment and path. Resolution produces [`ResolvedSelectedCapabilityRoot`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/exec-server/src/resolved_capability.rs:12), which holds the current ready `Environment` and exposes its executor filesystem.

The code explicitly says the resolved form is process-local and must not be persisted.

- **Origin:** Raw selections enter through thread extension initialization or resumed history. Resolution captures the currently ready environment instance.

- **Propagation:** Resolved roots enter the exact [`StepContext`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/session/step_context.rs:14). Raw descriptors also feed skill and MCP environment providers.

- **Expected boundary:** The stable descriptor may cross persistence boundaries; the live resolved capability may not. A resolved root must stay bound to the step and exact environment instance in which it was resolved.

- **Compiling misuse:** Internal code can retain a cloned resolved root in session-global state across steps, despite the documented prohibition. Code can also manufacture a public raw descriptor and arrange for it to be resolved, unless the insertion boundary independently validates selection authority.

- **Required representation:** Two phases:

  `Selected(root_id, environment_id, path)`
  `→ Resolved(root_id, environment_instance, step)`

  The resolved phase needs a `no_persist`, `no_cross_step`, and exact-environment constraint.

- **Beyond security:** This is snapshot consistency. The same analysis could prevent ready-state handles, configuration snapshots, or cache views from escaping the epoch in which they are valid.

### 5. `AgentControl`: task-creation and inter-agent mutation authority

[`AgentControl`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/agent/control.rs:175) explicitly describes itself as the capability to spawn agents and perform inter-agent communication. It owns access to the registry, execution limiter, task coordinator, and thread-manager state.

- **Origin:** [`ThreadManager::agent_control`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/thread_manager.rs:1577) constructs it from the manager’s global state.

- **Propagation:** It is stored in session services, cloned into child sessions, and used by multi-agent tool handlers. Its methods spawn, interrupt, resume, and send messages.

- **Expected boundary:** A control handle should remain within one root agent tree. User-triggered spawning should pass through the authorized tool handler, and target or parent agent IDs should belong to the same tree as the handle.

- **Compiling misuse:** Any internal code holding `session.services.agent_control` can call `spawn_agent_with_metadata` directly, bypassing the handler-level `spawn_is_authorized` check. An unbranded handle can also be combined with IDs or configuration from another session tree.

- **Required representation:** `AgentControl(root_session, registry_instance)` with effect summaries such as `Spawn`, `Message`, and `Interrupt`; same-root constraints on agent IDs; and explicit attenuation for read-only versus mutating operations.

- **Beyond security:** This enforces architecture and ownership: a child task must be created by the coordinator responsible for its lifecycle, quota, and shutdown.

### 6. Outgoing message senders: publication authority

[`OutgoingMessageSender`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/app-server/src/outgoing_message.rs:104) is process-wide publication authority. `ThreadScopedOutgoingMessageSender` wraps it with a thread ID and selected connection sets.

- **Origin:** The raw sender is constructed from the app-server transport channel. Thread-scoped senders are constructed from the raw sender, connection lists, and a thread ID.

- **Propagation:** The scoped wrapper is cloneable and passed through event-processing code. It can publish thread notifications, send requests to selected connections, or deliberately escape to global notification methods.

- **Expected boundary:** A scoped sender’s connection set should come from an authoritative subscription snapshot. Thread events should remain bound to the wrapper’s thread. Experimental messages should only reach the corresponding feature-enabled subset.

- **Compiling misuse:** App-server code can construct a scoped wrapper containing an unrelated active connection and send it another thread’s event. The lower layer verifies connection liveness, but the caller supplies the claimed recipient set. Thread-local code can also invoke the global-notification escape hatch.

- **Required representation:** An attenuated capability
  `Publisher(thread, allowed_connections, feature_subsets)`, with subset checks on derivation and `event.thread == publisher.thread`.

- **Beyond security:** This verifies event topology, tenant isolation, feature-cohort consistency, and correct response routing.

### 7. `StateDbHandle`: broad persistent-state authority

[`StateDbHandle`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/rollout/src/state_db.rs:27) is an `Arc<StateRuntime>`. The runtime exposes thread archival/deletion, goals, jobs, memories, logs, and other durable operations.

- **Origin:** Rollout initialization opens the configured state database and creates the runtime.

- **Propagation:** The handle is cloned through the app server, thread manager, session services, and `CodexThread`; [`CodexThread::state_db`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/core/src/codex_thread.rs:738) returns another clone.

- **Expected boundary:** Raw database authority should remain in infrastructure or repository layers. Session-facing code should ideally receive subject-scoped operations rather than an unrestricted runtime.

- **Compiling misuse:** Code with a `CodexThread` can obtain the database handle and call a method such as `delete_thread` with another thread’s ID. The handle is associated informally with the current thread, while the mutation target is an independent parameter.

- **Required representation:** Database identity, effect kind (`Read`, `Write`, `Delete`, `Close`), and subject key. A thread accessor should attenuate the raw capability to `ThreadStateDb(current_thread)`, with `target_thread == capability_thread`.

- **Beyond security:** This becomes an architectural-cut rule: domain and tool layers must not acquire unrestricted storage handles, even if their current code only performs reads.

### 8. Lifecycle witnesses that act as capabilities

Three values are especially revealing because their authority is temporal rather than conventionally “privileged.”

#### `TurnOriginReservation`

[`TurnOriginReservation`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/app-server/src/thread_state.rs:63) reserves a turn-to-connection mapping. Consuming `commit(self)` preserves it; `Drop` rolls it back.

A compiling misuse is to commit before submission succeeds, move it into an unrelated task, or `mem::forget` it. Required facts are affine typestate and ordering: `Reserved → Committed | RolledBack`, with commit only after the associated submission.

#### `InFlightTaskClaim`

[`InFlightTaskClaim`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/app-server/src/thread_state.rs:46) returns a unit-like `Claimed` variant, while release separately accepts thread and turn IDs.

A compiling misuse is to treat one claim as authority for a different task or release a different `(thread, turn)`. It needs an owner-bound representation such as `Claim(fingerprint, thread, turn)` and an exactly-once release/finalization obligation.

#### `EvictionCompletion`

[`EvictionCompletion`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/app-server/src/thread_lifecycle.rs:16) signals completion by sending from `Drop`. It propagates into a spawned unload task and should only be dropped after shutdown and unload bookkeeping finish.

Dropping it early falsely reports completion; forgetting it leaves a waiter hanging. It needs an exact eviction/thread subject, a postcondition before completion, and Drop/unwind/cancellation modeling.

These are not primarily security checks. They are protocol, lifecycle, and causal-completion checks—strong evidence that capability flow generalizes well beyond access control.

## An important non-candidate

[`SkillAuthority`](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/ext/skills/src/catalog.rs:40) looks like a capability but is better classified as a claim or selector. It is publicly constructible, and the read boundary validates its authority kind and package against the per-turn enabled catalog.

This matters for discovery: kda should not classify values by names such as `Authority`, `Token`, or `Permit`. A value is a capability when possession plus acceptance at a sink supplies effective authority. A forgeable identifier that is revalidated against authoritative state is not, by itself, that authority.

## Static model kda would need

A useful core fact would resemble:

```text

    subject,
```

Contracts would declare:

- Trusted mint sites.

Relational facts are essential: many properties compare the capability’s subject with a separate request, thread ID, connection set, or process.

Kda already has much of the substrate:

The missing piece is not another name-based lint. It is a shared capability contract layer joining provenance, authority lattices, relational subjects, escape analysis, and lifecycle obligations.

## What this proves beyond a call graph

The strongest examples all contain identical call edges with different correctness:

| Call edge | Legitimate value | Illegitimate value |
|---|---|---|
| `ToolRuntime::run` | attempt derived by the orchestrator | hand-built or widened attempt |
| handler `handle` | router-minted invocation | reconstructed invocation |
| publish notification | authoritative subscriber subset | caller-invented recipient set |
| `delete_thread` | handle scoped to that thread | unrelated thread’s broad DB handle |
| spawn agent | current-root authorized `AgentControl` | handle from another tree or bypass path |
| drop completion guard | after causal work completes | before shutdown or bookkeeping |

An ordinary call graph reports all six as reachable. Even a context-sensitive call graph does not establish value origin, same-subject equality, authority ordering, attenuation, exact ownership, or temporal state. Once those facts are added, the analysis has become capability/data-flow reasoning rather than call-graph reasoning.

So the answer is a fairly strong **yes**: capability flow would give kda a unifying model for security authority, architectural ownership, snapshot validity, event topology, and lifecycle correctness. The most promising first targets are `ToolInvocation`, `SandboxAttempt`, resolved capability roots, `AgentControl`, scoped publishers, and the owner-bound lifecycle witnesses.

## Conclusion

Yes—semantic API compatibility could become a major standalone `cargo kda` mode, but only if it is framed as an evidence-producing compatibility analyzer, not a universal proof that every possible downstream crate remains compatible.

The strongest opportunity is beyond public-item inventories:

- Compare resolved types, predicates, trait obligations, impl applicability, auto-trait behavior, effective public paths, and configuration worlds.
- Recompile known downstream crates against both revisions and compare method resolution and inferred types.
- Generate and compile focused downstream witnesses for suspected open-world breakage.
- Report uncertainty explicitly where dependencies, procedural macros, build scripts, or unknown downstream code prevent proof.

A semantic mode would be differentiated from `cargo-semver-checks`, which already covers a large syntactic/structural lint set but currently documents gaps in type changes, generics/lifetimes, feature subsets, and cross-crate items. It already uses witness compilation for cases where metadata queries alone are insufficient. [cargo-semver-checks’ documented limitations and witnesses](https://github.com/obi1kenobi/cargo-semver-checks), [Rust Project Goal on type and cross-crate limitations](https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-project-goals/2025h2/cargo-semver-checks.html).

## Classification model

“Definitely breaking” should mean that KDA can demonstrate a legal downstream program that compiled against the baseline and fails against the new revision. It does not mean every downstream crate breaks.

The mode should distinguish:

- **Definitely breaking:** proven by semantic set comparison, compiler diagnostic, or compiled witness.
- **Potentially breaking:** legal downstream circumstances could break, but the actual downstream imports, impls, calls, or assumptions are unknown.
- **Compatible in analyzed worlds:** the relevant compiler-observable acceptance relationship was preserved for every analyzed configuration.
- **Unknown:** evidence collection was incomplete. Unknown must never be collapsed into compatible.

Source compatibility and behavioral significance should be separate axes. Cargo’s own SemVer guide notes that nearly any change can alter runtime behavior, even though its compatibility guidance mostly concerns compilation. [Cargo SemVer compatibility guide](https://doc.rust-lang.org/beta/cargo/reference/semver.html).

## Recommended analysis architecture

A credible mode should use three evidence layers:

1. **Resolved API fact collection**

   Build an expanded effective-path graph and normalized type/trait/impl facts for each revision and configuration world. Rustdoc JSON could provide broad API inventory and interoperability with existing tools. Rust-analyzer HIR is suitable for navigation, identity, candidate generation, and known-workspace resolution.

2. **Compiler-backed confirmation**

   Encode suspected breaks as small downstream witness crates. Compile each witness against the baseline and current revisions under the same toolchain, dependency cohort, target, and features. Rustc should be the authority for trait solving, coherence, inference, and method ambiguity.

   If deeper extraction is eventually required, a versioned compiler fact producer could expose normalized predicates and impl facts. It should be isolated behind a schema because rustc-private APIs are costly to maintain.

3. **Known-downstream validation**

   For a workspace such as `codex-rs`, rebuild actual dependent crates against both revisions and retain call-resolution and inferred-substitution deltas. This gives much stronger evidence than an abstract open-world warning and is an especially good fit for KDA’s agent-oriented model.

Every result must name its world:

`toolchain × target × feature set × dependency resolution × cfg inputs`

KDA’s current default of all features is useful for discovery but insufficient for compatibility because it cannot represent all feature subsets or negative cfg paths: [workspace.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/workspace.rs:186).

## Standalone-mode recommendation

A separate mode—conceptually `cargo kda compat --base <revision>`—is justified. It should not merely add more families to the existing `diff`, because compatibility needs different:

- Revision and package selection.
- Target, feature, toolchain, and dependency matrices.
- Certainty semantics and exit codes.
- Downstream witness artifacts.
- Version-policy configuration.
- Distinction between source, coherence, resolution, and behavioral effects.

A sensible scope progression would be:

1. Effective public paths, traits, enums, explicit impls, auto-trait loss, feature/MSRV worlds, and existing structural SemVer checks.
2. Predicate implication, associated projections, blanket-impl overlap, conditional auto traits, and dyn compatibility.
3. Compiler-generated witnesses and recompilation of known downstream workspaces.
4. Method-target and inference deltas for actual consumers, plus KDA’s bounded behavioral contracts.

The mode becomes a major standalone capability at stages 2–3. Stage 1 alone would mostly duplicate `cargo-semver-checks` and would not justify KDA’s semantic positioning.

The defensible product claim would be:

> KDA finds compiler-confirmed compatibility breaks, analyzes semantic hazards that ordinary API inventories cannot see, validates known downstream crates, and exposes precisely which unexamined worlds prevent a compatibility conclusion.

It should not claim universal Rust source or behavioral compatibility. That claim is blocked by the open downstream universe, dependency variation, macro/build-script execution, solver complexity, and general program equivalence.

## Conclusion

Yes. `kda` can statically verify meaningful portions of unsafe Rust contracts—but the defensible unit of verification is a specific safety obligation at a specific operation, not an entire `unsafe` block.

A useful result would read:

> The initialized-range obligation for this `Vec::from_raw_parts` call is discharged on every reachable path, assuming contract version X and target Y.

It should never read:

> This unsafe block is sound.

That distinction matters because Rust still does not have a fully specified unsafe-memory model; even the Reference says its undefined-behavior list is not exhaustive and that aliasing rules are not completely determined. [Rust Reference: undefined behavior](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/reference/behavior-considered-undefined.html)

This approach fits `kda` well. The repository already has exact semantic identities, bounded cross-function reasoning, explicit unknown states, FFI protocol analyses, raw-resource ownership, unsafe precondition boundaries, and Drop-effect summaries. The relevant foundations are visible in [unsafe_precondition_boundary.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/unsafe_precondition_boundary.rs:1), [unsafe_contracts.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/unsafe_contracts.rs:1), and the [MIR fact protocol](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/docs/mir-fact-protocol.md:46).

## Result semantics

Every rule should produce one of these outcomes:

- **Verified**: every premise of a versioned contract was established by a sound supported analysis, with no relevant precision loss.
- **Violated**: a feasible path or unconditional structural fact demonstrably contradicts the contract.
- **Possible violation**: heuristic evidence suggests a defect, but path feasibility, aliasing, or an external premise remains uncertain.
- **Unknown**: evidence escaped, a call or operation was unresolved, an external contract was absent, the proof domain was insufficient, or an analysis bound was reached.
- **Not applicable**.

Missing proof must not automatically become a warning. On explicitly enrolled critical surfaces, it may become a policy finding—“this obligation could not be verified”—but not a claim of unsoundness.

Comments, variable names, tests, `debug_assert!`, and a `// SAFETY:` explanation are useful review evidence, but are not proof. A runtime check becomes proof only when it dominates the operation, refers to the same semantic value or allocation, uses overflow-safe arithmetic, and applies on all relevant normal and cleanup paths.

## Candidate contract families

### 1. Pointer validity, extent, and alignment

**Invariant:** A pointer is live, has acceptable provenance, is aligned for the accessed type, and covers the complete byte range accessed. Required non-nullness and `isize::MAX` restrictions also hold.

**Semantic evidence:**

- Origin from `Box`, `Vec`, a slice, an allocation, or a reference.
- Allocation identity and live/deallocated state.
- `len`, `capacity`, byte offsets, element sizes, and checked arithmetic.
- Dominating bounds, null, and alignment checks.
- Reallocation, deallocation, and pointer-escape events.

Known-short extents, unchecked arithmetic overflow, free-before-use, and provably misaligned offsets can contradict the contract.

**Proof boundary:** Sound verification is achievable for nonescaped allocation tokens and affine or bounded integer ranges. Pointers reconstructed from integers, opaque FFI, exposed provenance, unknown callees, or arbitrary heap aliases normally require `unknown`.

**Generalization:** This applies across dereferences, `ptr::read`/`write`, copy operations, slice construction, raw-part reconstruction, and FFI buffers—not just one unsafe API.

This is a strong near-term target because [ffi_buffer_bounds.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/ffi_buffer_bounds.rs:1) and the unsafe-precondition machinery already represent much of the required vocabulary.

### 2. Aliasing and non-overlap

**Invariant:** Exclusive accesses do not overlap other live accesses, shared accesses are not mutated outside permitted mechanisms, and APIs requiring disjoint ranges receive disjoint ranges.

**Semantic evidence:**

- Allocation identity plus byte intervals.
- Origins from `&mut`, `split_at_mut`, or other disjointness-preserving operations.
- Dominating non-overlap checks.
- Active leases and their end points.
- `UnsafeCell`, synchronization, pointer escapes, and unknown calls.

Provably overlapping intervals supplied to a non-overlapping operation can be a sound contradiction.

**Proof boundary:** A sound positive result is realistic only inside a closed interval domain with controlled escapes and modeled callees. General absence of aliasing should not be claimed: Rust’s precise aliasing model remains unsettled.

**Unknown handling:** Integer round trips, opaque calls, stored raw pointers, dynamic dispatch, and heap graph ambiguity should normally stop the proof. An overlap suggested only by an over-approximated path is a possible violation, not a violation.

**Generalization:** Useful for raw copies, manual slices, custom containers, FFI buffers, and internal borrowing abstractions.

This should be deliberately narrow rather than an attempt to build a universal borrow checker for raw pointers.

### 3. Initialization and type validity

**Invariant:** Every byte or element read has been initialized, and its bit pattern is valid for the interpreted type. Ownership-bearing values are not duplicated through repeated reads.

**Semantic evidence:**

- `MaybeUninit::write`, field writes, and initialized-element prefixes.
- Success codes from modeled FFI out-parameter contracts.
- Branch-sensitive discriminants or runtime tags.
- `set_len`, `assume_init`, `assume_init_read`, and drop state.
- Validity constraints for references, booleans, enums, and non-zero types.

An `assume_init` reachable before all required writes, a duplicated `assume_init_read`, or `set_len` beyond the initialized prefix can be a sound violation. The standard documentation explicitly places these validity obligations on `MaybeUninit` users. [MaybeUninit documentation](https://doc.rust-lang.org/core/mem/union.MaybeUninit.html)

**Proof boundary:** Definite initialization of fields, constant indices, and monotonic initialized prefixes is tractable. Full arbitrary bit-validity proof is not; support it only for modeled type constructors.

**Unknown handling:** Opaque writers, symbolic indices outside the range domain, unmodeled unions, and uncertain FFI success semantics produce `unknown`.

**Generalization:** FFI out parameters, array construction, `Vec` spare capacity, placement APIs, unions, and custom containers.

This is especially promising. `codex-rs` already contains a clean success-gated example in [workspace.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/agent-task-store/src/workspace.rs:699) and an initialized-prefix construction in [socket.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/shell-escalation/src/unix/socket.rs:95).

### 4. Layout, representation, and ABI

**Invariant:** Size, alignment, field offsets, ABI, and destination validity match the operation. Deallocation uses the original allocation layout.

**Semantic evidence:**

- Target-specific rustc layout facts.
- `repr(C)`, `repr(transparent)`, `repr(packed)`, and enum representation.
- `size_of`, `align_of`, offsets, and allocator `Layout`.
- FFI header size/version fields.
- Source and destination type validity requirements.

A known alignment mismatch, reference to a packed field, incompatible ABI, or different allocation/deallocation layout can be a sound contradiction.

**Proof boundary:** Layout equality is not enough to establish type validity, ownership, or provenance. Rust-representation field order is generally unspecified, so target/compiler assumptions must be explicit. [Rust Reference: type layout](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/reference/type-layout.html)

**Unknown handling:** Unspecified layout, platform-dependent definitions, opaque C declarations, and unsupported target configurations become `unknown`.

**Generalization:** Transmutation, typed pointer casts, custom allocation, FFI structs, tagged unions, and raw-part reconstruction.

### 5. Lifetimes hidden behind raw pointers

**Invariant:** The pointee and its backing storage remain valid for every foreign or deferred use. Any reference reconstructed from the pointer is not assigned a lifetime longer than the actual owner.

**Semantic evidence:**

- Pointer origin from a local, field, slice, or owned allocation.
- Whether the owner is stored alongside the pointer.
- `PhantomData` ownership and variance signals.
- Registration, deregistration, join, completion, and cancellation operations.
- Reallocation or movement of backing storage.
- Project-level scope types and retaining constructors.

Owner destruction before a required terminal event, returning a reference with an unconstrained lifetime, or retaining a pointer into movable storage can contradict the contract.

**Proof boundary:** This can be sound when the foreign retention behavior is supplied as an authoritative contract and temporal ordering is established. Inferring the contract merely from naming, field proximity, or `PhantomData` is heuristic.

**Unknown handling:** Unspecified foreign retention, escaped context pointers, unmodeled callbacks, and detached background work become `unknown`.

**Generalization:** Callback contexts, async OS I/O, registries, intrusive structures, caches, and self-referential wrappers.

Yuga demonstrates that lifetime-annotation mismatches plus field-sensitive alias analysis can find real bugs, but its inferred relationships are better treated as review leads than universal proofs. [Yuga paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08507)

### 6. Ownership reconstruction and deallocation

**Invariant:** A raw resource has exactly one active owner. Reconstruction uses the original pointer, length, capacity, allocator, and layout; ownership is reconstructed exactly once; no prior owner remains live.

For example, `Vec::from_raw_parts` requires the original allocation, compatible alignment/layout, initialized `len`, `len <= capacity`, and exclusive ownership. [Vec::from_raw_parts](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/std/vec/struct.Vec.html#method.from_raw_parts)

**Semantic evidence:**

- `into_raw`, `ManuallyDrop`, or FFI-acquire origin.
- A provenance tuple containing allocation, length, capacity, allocator family, and layout.
- Moves, transfers, releases, reconstruction, and subsequent uses.
- Branch-sensitive linear ownership state.

Double reconstruction, use after transfer, allocator mismatch, `len > capacity`, or retaining the original owner can be sound violations.

**Proof boundary:** Linear tokens and exact tuple identity work well until values are copied, aliased, decomposed into opaque storage, or cross unmodeled FFI.

**Unknown handling:** Those escape conditions should stop verification rather than guess tuple identity.

**Generalization:** `Box`, `Vec`, `Arc`, `CString`, file descriptors, handles, custom arenas, and foreign resources.

The existing [raw_resource_ownership.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/raw_resource_ownership.rs:1) is already close to this model.

### 7. Manual `Send` and `Sync`

**Invariant:** Moving a value between threads, or sharing references to it, cannot introduce data races, use-after-free, or thread-affinity violations. Generic parameters and destructor behavior must also satisfy the claim.

**Semantic evidence:**

- rustc auto-trait results and negative implementations.
- Field types and generic bounds.
- `Rc`, `Cell`, `UnsafeCell`, locks, atomics, and synchronization.
- Raw-pointer pointee ownership and retention.
- Thread-affine external handles.
- Drop effects and which thread may execute them.

An unbounded generic payload that is transferred, reachable `Rc`, or an unsynchronized shared `UnsafeCell` can sometimes provide a sound contradiction. Raw pointers alone are not proof of unsoundness; the Nomicon explains that their negative auto-trait behavior is partly a guard against accidental derivation. [Send and Sync, Rustonomicon](https://doc.rust-lang.org/nightly/nomicon/send-and-sync.html)

**Proof boundary:** “All fields are `Send`” is insufficient for a type whose safety depends on logical ownership, callbacks, or foreign thread rules. Positive verification requires explicit models for those semantics.

**Unknown handling:** Unmodeled handles, opaque raw pointees, callbacks, and thread-affine destruction become `unknown`.

**Generalization:** Every manual `unsafe impl Send` or `Sync`.

`kda` has a three-way invariant engine in [unsafe_trait_invariants.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Documents/ChatGPT/kd-analzyer/src/unsafe_trait_invariants.rs:1), but its major missing piece is an automatic semantic evidence collector. Clippy’s `non_send_fields_in_send_ty` is explicitly heuristic, which leaves room for a more contract-oriented analysis. [Clippy lint documentation](https://rust-lang.github.io/rust-clippy/stable/index.html#non_send_fields_in_send_ty)

### 8. FFI protocol contracts

**Invariant:** Both the declaration-level ABI and the dynamic protocol are respected:

- Out parameters are read only after documented success.
- Buffers are sufficiently large and correctly aligned.
- Nullable and non-null arguments follow the external contract.
- Runtime tags guard the matching union projection.
- Size and version headers are populated.
- Retained pointers remain live until completion.
- Ownership and release-family conventions match.
- Unwinding does not cross an incompatible ABI boundary.

**Semantic evidence:**

- Reviewed binding metadata or imported headers.
- Exact foreign-function identity and platform version.
- Dominating status checks and same-place tag checks.
- Length, capacity, layout, and pointer-origin facts.
- Completion, cancellation, deregistration, and release operations.
- `catch_unwind`, ABI kind, and no-unwind summaries.

**Proof boundary:** The Rust side can be proved only relative to an authoritative external contract. The Rust declaration itself does not prove what the foreign implementation does.

**Unknown handling:** Missing contract metadata, wrong platform version, dynamically loaded symbols, callbacks with unknown behavior, or opaque foreign retention become `unknown`.

**Generalization:** Declarative contract schemas make this reusable across API families.

This is already `kda`’s strongest differentiator: it has separate analyses for out parameters, counted buffers, runtime tags, struct headers, callbacks, and retained pointers. They should ultimately share one obligation language and fact ledger.

### 9. `Pin` guarantees

**Invariant:** Once a `!Unpin` value is pinned, it remains valid at the same address until its destructor completes. Structurally pinned fields are not moved or replaced, storage is not reused early, and unsafe projections preserve pinning.

These requirements include destructor and storage behavior, not merely “the address did not visibly change.” [std::pin documentation](https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/pin/)

**Semantic evidence:**

- Pin creation and owner identity.
- `Unpin` and `PhantomPinned`.
- Unsafe projections and declared structurally pinned fields.
- `mem::replace`, `take`, `swap`, `ptr::read`, container reallocation, and raw copies.
- `repr(packed)`.
- `drop_in_place`, deallocation, reuse, and foreign completion state.

Unconditional `Unpin` for a structurally pinned type, unsafe projection followed by replacement, packed pinned fields, or early `Pin::into_inner_unchecked` can be sound contradictions under an explicit structural-pinning contract.

**Proof boundary:** Local contradictions are tractable. Proving “this value is never moved anywhere” requires complete escape and reachable-method analysis; absent that, return `unknown`.

**Generalization:** Futures, intrusive structures, self-referential objects, and asynchronous foreign I/O.

The Windows overlapped-I/O object in [windows_pipe.rs](/C:/Users/kuh/Desktop/kd4/codex-rs/tui/src/ide_context/windows_pipe.rs:165) is an excellent integration fixture because its Pin guarantee interacts with FFI retention, cancellation, `Send`, and destruction.

### 10. Destructor, cleanup, and unwind behavior

**Invariant:** Ownership, initialization, and pinning invariants remain valid on normal and cleanup paths. Temporarily duplicated ownership is restored before any possible unwind; initialized elements are dropped before storage reuse; foreign resources are released in the required order.

**Semantic evidence:**

- MIR cleanup edges, Drop terminators, and drop flags.
- `ptr::read`, `ManuallyDrop`, and temporary duplicated ownership.
- Calls that may panic or unwind.
- Guard typestate and commit/rollback operations.
- Initialized-prefix changes and deallocation order.
- FFI ABI and unwind contracts.

A possible panic between ownership duplication and restoration, deallocation before `drop_in_place`, or foreign release before its dependent fields can become a sound violation when the path is feasible.

**Proof boundary:** Positive verification needs complete unwind/effect summaries for all relevant callees. Otherwise only local contradictions or possible violations are justified.

**Unknown handling:** Unresolved calls, unknown panic behavior, custom Drop through dynamic dispatch, or omitted cleanup MIR become `unknown`.

**Generalization:** Custom collections, exception-safety guards, Pin, FFI wrappers, and ownership reconstruction.

Rudra established the usefulness of static panic-safety and manual `Send`/`Sync` patterns, although its released tooling is tied to an older nightly and has practical workspace limitations. [Rudra](https://github.com/sslab-gatech/Rudra)

## Comparison with existing tools

| Tool | What it establishes | What remains for `kda` |
|---|---|---|
| rustc | Safe-language typing, borrow checking, auto traits, selected invalid-value/ABI lints, explicit unsafe operations | It assumes unsafe preconditions and manual unsafe-trait promises; `unsafe_op_in_unsafe_fn` enforces visibility, not correctness. [Edition Guide](https://doc.rust-lang.org/edition-guide/rust-2024/unsafe-op-in-unsafe-fn.html) |
| Clippy | Useful syntactic and HIR bug patterns | Its raw-pointer and manual-`Send` checks document important false negatives and heuristics; it does not discharge protocol obligations. |
| Miri | Excellent detection of executed UAF, OOB, uninitialized reads, alignment failures, data races, and experimental aliasing violations | It observes particular executions, inputs, layouts, and interleavings; most FFI is unsupported, and a clean run is not a proof. [Miri README](https://github.com/rust-lang/miri/blob/master/README.md) |
| Rudra | Panic-safety, higher-order invariant, and manual `Send`/`Sync` patterns | Useful precedent, but narrower and operationally dated. |
| Yuga | Hidden lifetime and alias relationships | Strong inspiration for heuristic lifetime leads, not a complete unsafe verifier. |
| RAPx | The closest broad static unsafe verifier: alias/dataflow reasoning, ranges, ownership, contracts, and SMT-backed checks | `kda` should complement it with deterministic project contracts, FFI lifecycles, Pin, and repository policy rather than duplicate its general solver. Its current checklist still identifies Pin verification as unsupported. [RAPx](https://github.com/safer-rust/RAPx) |
| Kani | Exhaustive bounded model checking within a supplied harness and bound | Requires harnesses/specifications and does not fully model lifetimes, concurrency, every intrinsic, or all ABI/unwind behavior. [Kani UB coverage](https://model-checking.github.io/kani/undefined-behaviour.html) |

A sensible toolchain is therefore:

- rustc and Clippy for universal baseline checks.
- `kda` for repository-wide contract closure on configured unsafe surfaces.
- Miri for executed dynamic semantics.
- RAPx or Kani for deep analysis of selected unsafe kernels.

## Recommended capability roadmap

### Highest-value additions

1. **A versioned unsafe-obligation catalog**

   Cover selected core/std operations plus project and binding contracts. Contracts should be keyed by exact semantic identity, target, crate/compiler version, features, and ABI—not source spelling.

2. **A unified MIR safety-fact ledger**

   Track:

   - Allocation identity and live state.
   - Byte intervals and alignment.
   - Initialized fields or prefixes.
   - Linear ownership state.
   - Foreign-retention deadlines.
   - Target layout.
   - Pin state.
   - Drop and unwind state.

3. **Prioritize five contract families**

   Start with initialization, ownership reconstruction, pointer extent/alignment, FFI protocols, and Drop/unwind. These have strong semantic evidence, useful contradictions, and substantial existing `kda` infrastructure.

4. **Automatic `Send`/`Sync` evidence collection**

   Combine auto-trait queries, generic bounds, field capabilities, raw-pointer ownership contracts, synchronization, and thread-affine Drop effects. Do not treat raw pointers as intrinsically suspicious.

5. **Explicit structural-pinning contracts**

   Let a type declare which fields are structurally pinned and which terminal events permit extraction or deallocation. Check projections, moves, replacement, and cleanup against that declaration.

6. **A deliberately bounded alias domain**

   Prove interval disjointness and local exclusive leases. Do not attempt a universal raw-pointer alias or provenance proof.

### Reporting requirements

Every verified result should include:

- The precise operation and discharged obligation.
- The contract source and version.
- The relevant target/profile/features.
- The semantic evidence used.
- Assumptions about opaque or foreign code.
- Any proof-domain limits.

Every unknown result should name the exact precision loss: pointer escape, unresolved call, unsupported arithmetic, missing external contract, target mismatch, analysis cap, or incomplete cleanup information.

### Validation strategy

Use mutation-based fixtures from the real target:

- Move `assume_init` outside its success guard.
- Change initialized-prefix equality to a weaker comparison.
- Reconstruct a raw owner twice.
- Drop backing storage before foreign completion.
- Remove a required generic `Send` bound.
- Replace or move a structurally pinned field.
- Introduce a panicking call between `ptr::read` and ownership restoration.

Measure verified, violated, possible, and unknown cases independently. Unknown-rate reduction is important, but it must never be achieved by silently converting assumptions into facts.

The resulting product would offer genuine static value: not “unsafe detection,” and not a universal soundness claim, but repeatable verification that concrete, reusable safety obligations are closed—or precisely explaining why they could not be. No implementation changes were made.
